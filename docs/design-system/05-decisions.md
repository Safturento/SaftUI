# Decisions

Answers the owner has given to the questions and discrepancies. Each stands as a rule from its date; the pages below it (Discrepancies, Questions, Code changes) and the components are updated as the work lands, and until then this page wins where they differ.

## 2026-11-13

1. **The options window follows the theme.** Its controls' lines follow the theme's (Thin's under Thin, a recoloured Thick's under that) while fields stay dark, and the in-world close button is drawn in its window's theme. (Question 1, option C.)
2. **Panels are see-through or solid across the whole UI from one theme setting, and `/sui` follows it too.** The theme's Background carries the opacity; every panel follows it, the options window included; an individual panel can still override. (New.)
3. **A colour and its opacity are one control.** Wherever a setting has both a colour and an opacity today (two rows, or a colour beside an Alpha slider), they become one colour control whose picker sets the opacity, as the theme's Background does. (New; the pairs still to convert are listed under Code changes once audited.)
4. **One button, one size.** A single button component for `/sui` and the world, at one height and in one font; no second size. The edit mode footer's mixed sizes and fonts are the example of what this ends. (Question 2, stricter than either option.)
5. **Edit mode's controls leave `/sui`'s footer** for a bar of their own across the top of the screen while edit mode is on, with toggles for which modules' movers show. Built: the bar, docked or floating, with Undo, Redo, Reset and a History list. (New.)
6. **The `/sui` window collapses:** its sidebar can be collapsed, and the window shrinks to a half-width, one-column size and then to a small floating bar, and grows back. Being designed (same mockup). (New.)
7. **"On" is the accent colour,** never green text. **The accent is a theme colour** the player can change, with its own swatch in the Theme section. Built: Theme > Accent (color roles, Task 2). (Question 3, option A, plus the swatch.)
8. **`/sui` tooltips stay Blizzard's tooltip frame, with the description in SaftUI's normal text colour** in place of Blizzard's gold. (Question on tooltips.)
9. **The colour control's code is one colour everywhere.** Not black or white by brightness. The control is being redrawn with the code beside the swatch on the control's own dark field (mockup, part 1). (Question on the hex code's legibility.)
10. **Resets are per setting:** a `rotate-ccw` icon beside a control whose value differs from its default; no section or page Reset buttons. Built in the kit as the `default` option field. (Was already a rule; confirmed as general.)
11. **SaftUI scales with the display:** automatic from the screen's height against 1440, with a "UI size" slider to override; borders in whole pixels. A project, after the tokens are in code. (New; see the repo's followups.)
12. **The pixel font is likely to go** in favour of a face that reads well small at any scale; candidates are being compared in game (`/suifonts`). (New; undecided which.)

## 2026-11-15, UI scaling, phase 1

56. **The UI size is automatic: the screen's height over 1440, snapped to a whole number within 5%, times a UI size of 70% to 150% (default 100%).** The design's three questions, answered with the recommendations: "Good to go with those recommendations". Every install starts in Automatic at 100%; a friend whose tuned layout no longer suits has `/sui scale pixel`. The default and the range were shipped without the friends' check ("if it's just fine tuning that would change we can get feedback from him later"), so a one-line change retunes them.
57. **Three modes: Automatic, Pixel for pixel (every size that many pixels, as before) and The game's UI scale.** The choice is saved per account on this computer and never in a profile, so a shared profile keeps each person's screen size.
58. **Every line is a whole number of pixels: one up to k 1.49, two from 1.5 (marks two lines, a Thick frame three).** Texture snapping is turned off on the lines SaftUI draws, since a one-pixel line half a pixel off whole pixels can lose a side under the game's snapping (E5b). One-pixel lines stay one pixel on the frames the game scales (the Cooldown Manager and buffs at an Icon size other than 100%): "keep one-pixel lines on frames Blizzard scales".
59. **A UI size is tried through "Keep this UI size?", and `/sui scale reset` is the way back from any size.** The prompt counts 15 seconds, goes back on Esc, Go back or the time, and ignores its parent's scale so it is readable at any size; Keep takes no click for half a second. A size is applied when the slider is let go (the Range's `applyOnRelease`), not at every step.
60. **Fonts follow the scale unrounded, and Homespun stays (decision 41).** The Mac friend's check of Homespun and the Lucide icons at 1.55 decides whether phase 5 needs a rule; the font demo stays until then.
61. **Nothing saved is converted when the scale changes.** A frame is rounded to whole pixels where it is placed, so a saved position goes through a scale change and back unchanged. A nameplate's odd Width or Height is one pixel larger at the owner's scale (plates are an even number of pixels), which the owner was told.
62. **The rest is phased:** the options kit's boxes and the Keep prompt's buttons (phase 2), edit mode (phase 3), each addon (phase 4, in the order the friends look at them) and crispness on dense screens (phase 5). Until then the kit's controls are a fraction of a pixel off away from 1440p, and the Whole pixels rule in the README is the target.

## 2026-11-15, one Accent, every state derived

63. **One Accent, and every interaction state of every interactive widget is derived from it by rule.** The owner's rules, agreed on the mockup: hover changes the body, never only the border; pushed is the same, stronger; selected, checked or current is a full-strength accent border (a left bar on a row, an underline on a tab) and a fill that fades out across the widget; selected and hovered compose; keyboard focus is an accent border; disabled dims the whole widget; text on a derived fill keeps its color (Text > Normal, or what the state gives it) and never flips to a dark text: the black outline is enough (the owner, 2026-11-16, after Hover 60% on a gold Accent). Buttons > Normal, Hover, Pushed and Checked are gone (migration step 8); Buttons > Danger stays (a meaning, not a state), and so do the Accent's uses that are not a state (links, the chat prefix, progress fills, markers). Three derivations are built and selectable: **Layers** (the Accent at an opacity over the body), **Tones** (the Accent made lighter or darker in OKLCH, "this looks great, I like the way the Tones look") and **Hue-shifted tones** (the lighter tone turns warm and the darker cool, or the reverse). The approved defaults are **Tones**, Hover 16%, Pushed 32%, Selected fill 24%, Selected fade 70%, Disabled 40%, Lightness step 0.08, and for the hue-shifted mode Hue shift 20 degrees, Chroma change 10%, warm light and cool shadow ("yeah that default is good, if I want to change it once it's in game I'll let you know"); the controls live in the Accent row's dots menu, so a player who only changes the Accent gets the defaults, with a live strip of the states and a reset on each setting. The scroll bar thumb rests at #4d4d4d, an active ability and an equipped item both wear the Accent edge (told apart by the fade), and Buttons > Danger stays in the Buttons section; each is one constant to change after the owner sees it in game. This **supersedes decision 54** (Hover's one meaning and Checked's own color: there is no Hover color and no Checked color now; hover is the derived tint on every widget, a tint over the body at the Hover strength, `BLEND` on every highlight) and the strengths of **decisions 29** (two tint strengths: a hovered or selected row is the rule's Hover, and a selected object's fill is the rule's Selected fill) and **30** (the action buttons' hover: it is the rule's hover over the icon, no longer a white tint). The numbers are `state-*` tokens; `st:StateColor` is the code's one reader; a scan (`tests/core/states_scan_test.lua`) keeps anything from drawing a state by hand.
