# Edit mode and `/sui`

SaftUI has its own edit mode. It never adds frames to Blizzard's Edit Mode.
- `/sui edit` or the `/sui` "Edit mode" button toggles it. It also follows Blizzard's Edit Mode opening and closing.
- Registered frames get a draggable overlay. Arrow keys nudge 1px, Shift+arrows 10px.
- Where overlays overlap, a list by the cursor names every one under it and marks the one a click or drag gets; the rest dim. Alt brings the next one to the front (`EditMode/picker.lua`, like `/fstack`). Overlays share one strata, so their frame levels decide which is on top: each gets its own pair (backdrop one below), handed out rising by `EM:BringToFront`. Don't set an overlay's level anywhere else.
- Clicking a frame opens `/sui` at that frame's section.
- A grid shows behind the UI while edit mode is on and Grid is enabled (`EditMode/grid.lua`). Two center lines sit on UIParent's center, rounded to a physical pixel (`EM:GetGridCenter`), and the other lines repeat outward every Grid size (`EM.GridLinePositions`), each 1 physical pixel thick. Snapping reads the same two functions, so what's drawn is what snaps. The grid also redraws from its own frame's `OnSizeChanged` (the screen events can fire before UIParent resizes). The lines are pooled textures on a SaftUI frame at `BACKGROUND` strata that never takes the mouse.
- The `/sui` footer has Grid, Snap to grid and Snap to frames toggles (green text when on), and `-` / size / `+` buttons that step Grid size by 4 between 8 and 128 (`EditMode/settings.lua`). `EditMode/footer.lua` (`EM:AddFooterButton`, `EM:UpdateFooter`) puts them on the window's footer region (`st.OptionsWindow:GetFooter()`, between Reload UI and Close) while edit mode is on and `/sui` is open, and takes them off otherwise; the window's status line then starts clear of them, and a page's footer control keeps clear of them (`OW:SetFooterInset`; the footer control, below). Edit mode works with `/sui` closed; the buttons just don't show. The "Edit mode" tab of Global Settings in `/sui` holds the same toggles and a Grid size slider. The values are in the profile (`st.defaults.editmode`: `grid`, `grid_size`, `snap_grid`, `snap_frames`), and `EM:UpdateConfig` redraws the grid and toggles after a profile change.
- **The outline.** An overlay is drawn in SaftUI's theme (`'theme'`, `'solid'`, kind `control`, drawn as a small frame under Thick and Thin and bare under Blizzard): one 1px line (white, or blue with a blue tint when selected) `EM.OutlineOutset()` outside its rect, which is the theme's border, `st:ThemeInset('theme', 'solid')` (2px under Thick, between two dark lines; 1px under Thin, with none). That's where a SaftUI frame's own border line sits, so frames look as far apart in edit mode as out of it, under either theme. A frame whose border line sits elsewhere re-anchors its overlay so the line lands on it. Chat does this: its backdrop is `padding` outside the holder, so its overlay goes `padding - EM.OutlineOutset()` out (`CHT:UpdateFootprint`), or `0` under None. Don't color the overlay's dark lines; that widens the outline past the border. A theme recolor would wipe the line's white or blue, so `EM:OnColorsChanged('theme')` puts it back on every mover. Under Blizzard SaftUI draws no line or fill: `mover.lua` shows Blizzard's Edit Mode selection art (`st.ThemeArt.Control('mover')`) as textures of the overlay, the highlight kit on every mover and the selected kit on the selected one, and `SetBorderColor` swaps them. `EM.OutlineOutset()` is unchanged. The picker's list is kind `tooltip`.
- Dragging snaps (`EditMode/snap.lua`, wired into `EM:StartDrag` / `EM:UpdateDrag`). The dragged mover's outline (`EM.GetOutlineRect`: its overlay's rect plus `EM.OutlineOutset()`) has its left, middle and right (and bottom, middle and top) pulled onto the nearest line within `EM.SNAP_REACH` (8 UIParent units). Snap to grid uses the grid's lines (same `EM.GridLinePositions` and `EM:GetGridCenter`, so it works with the grid hidden). Snap to frames uses the screen's center lines and the outline edges and centers of every other mover whose overlay is shown and on screen; with both on, the nearest line wins per axis. Snapping only corrects the drag's `x_off`/`y_off` (converted by the ratio of effective scales, rounded with `st:RoundToPixel`), never the anchor. Holding Ctrl suspends it, and arrow-key nudges ignore it. Movers whose frame moves with the dragged one (`st:MovesWith`: anchored to it, directly or through other frames, or its children) are skipped as targets, since they would shift during the drag. The lines are computed once at drag start. `EM.GetRectInUI` returns nil for a frame with a secret edge or scale (the Minimap's frame is Blizzard's), and that mover is skipped.
- Drags and nudges stop at the screen edge margin (below): after snapping, `EM.MarginCorrection` (`mover.lua`) pulls the move back so the frame's rect (not its outline) stays as far in from each edge as the game's clamp keeps it (`st:GetScreenMargin`: the margin, plus a holder's reach), so the margin wins over a snap line and a drag stops where the frame does. A drag or nudge starts from where the frame shows, not where its anchor says (`st:GetClampShift`: the game shows a frame anchored into the margin at the margin), so what's stored is where it ends. A frame whose rect can't be read (a secret edge) gets no margin from a drag.
- It closes on combat and refuses to open in combat.

The spec is in `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-06-saftui-edit-mode-design.md`, and the plan (Tasks 1–15) in `docs/superpowers/plans/2026-11-06-saftui-edit-mode.md`. The grid and snapping have their own spec, `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-09-saftui-edit-mode-grid-snap-design.md`, and plan, `docs/superpowers/plans/2026-11-09-saftui-edit-mode-grid-snap.md`.

## Registering a mover

```lua
st:RegisterMover('key', {
	frame = holderOrFrame,        -- the overlay does SetAllPoints on this; it needs a real size
	label = 'Name',
	getPosition = function() return self.config.position end,   -- read through self.config at call time
	setPosition = function(pos) self.config.position = pos end,
	apply = function() self:ApplyPosition() end,                 -- can run in combat lockdown
	configPath = { 'ModuleName', 'section', 'general' },         -- keys from the /sui root to a leaf group
	blizzardFrame = nil,          -- true only when frame is Blizzard's own, with no holder (the Minimap)
})
```

- **`st:RegisterConfigMover(frame, spec)`** (`SaftUI-Core/EditMode/editmode.lua`) is that call when the position is just the `position` field of a table in the profile, which is nearly every mover. `spec = { key, config, apply, ...}`: `key` is the mover's key; `config` is a function returning the table that holds `position` (`function() return self.config[id] end`), called on every read and write, never once at registration, since a profile change replaces the profile's tables; `apply` as above. `getPosition` returns `config().position` and `setPosition(pos)` stores the table it's given there. Every other field (`label`, `configPath`, `blizzardFrame`, ...) goes to `st:RegisterMover` as it is, and it returns the mover. A mover that does more than that when it reads or writes (the experience bar migrates its position on read, the totem bar reads through `TB:GetPosition`) keeps its own `st:RegisterMover` closures.
- **`st:PlaceAtPosition(frame, position)`** (`SaftUI-Core/api.lua`) is `frame:ClearAllPoints()` then `frame:SetPoint(st:UnpackPoint(position))`, and nothing else: the offsets go as saved (not rounded, not made numbers), and the anchor is whatever `position.frame` holds, so a name is the game's to resolve and none anchors to the frame's parent. It doesn't queue: a frame that can't move now (protected, in combat) goes through `st:SafeReposition` around it. A site that rounds the offsets (`st:PlaceHolder`, the action bars' `AB:PlaceFrame`), resolves with `st:ResolveFrame` and falls back to `UIParent` (the buffs, the swing timers, the bags), makes the offsets numbers (the bags, edit mode's own `GetAnchor`), or does other work between the clear and the set (`AB:PlaceFrame` sizing from the unpacked point, a `pcall` fallback), keeps its own. The two groups differ in what the frame does in game, which is why there are no flags to pull them into one.
- **The screen edge margin.** `st:RegisterMover` keeps the frame inside it (`st:KeepInScreenMargin`, below), so `frame` is SaftUI's own frame or holder. A mover on a Blizzard frame moved with no holder (the Minimap) sets `blizzardFrame = true`: SaftUI never clamps a Blizzard frame, so only its drags and nudges keep it in.

- **Offsets.** Drags and nudges write pixel-rounded `x_off`/`y_off` into the table from `getPosition()`, call `setPosition`; `apply` runs from `EM:CommitPosition` (at drag end and on each nudge), not on the drag's ticks, which move the frame directly and, about every 0.1s, call `setPosition` only to keep an open `/sui` showing the live x/y. Drags keep the frame's anchor point and relative frame. Only the offsets change.
- **`apply`** must go through `st:SafeReposition`, because a secure frame anchored to yours makes it protected in combat.
- **`configPath`** must resolve in `Config:GetOptionsTable`. Module tables are keyed by module name. End the path at a leaf group, usually `general`, since selecting a parent that only holds subgroups shows an empty pane.
- **Holder pattern.** Use a SaftUI holder frame when the real frame:
  - can be empty or hidden (loot rolls, boss and party/raid groups, closed bag windows),
  - has no size (the micro menu container), or
  - belongs to Blizzard (swing timer, buffs, chat, the extra action button and zone ability).

  Size the holder from the config, place it from the profile, and anchor the real frame to it. The overlay then always has a footprint. `st:PlaceHolder(holder, key, getSize, getPosition)` (`SaftUI-Core/api.lua`) sizes and places a holder through `st:SafeReposition` under `key`, its offsets rounded to whole pixels, reading `getSize()` and `getPosition()` when it runs, so after combat too: chat, the tooltip and the totem bar use it. The buffs and swing timers place theirs without rounding the offsets, so they keep their own.

  A SaftUI frame that's only hidden, and still sized and placed from the profile while hidden, can be its own mover frame: the overlay follows its edges either way. SaftUI's action bars (`SaftUI-ActionBars/options.lua`) move their secure headers this way, also while a bar is disabled or hidden by its Visibility setting, and while the pet bar has no pet or the stance bar no forms (laid out as one button then, so the outline keeps a size). Label the outline when the frame may not be visible (`mover.label:SetText`), as the action bars and the swing timer do. The extra buttons' outlines (`SaftUI-ActionBars/options.lua`) always say when they show, as they mostly don't.
- **Taking over a Blizzard Edit Mode frame.** The swing timer bars (`SaftUI-Misc/Skinning/blizzard/Blizzard_SwingTimerPosition.lua`), buffs (`Blizzard_BuffFramePosition.lua`), chat (`SaftUI-Chat/chatframe.lua`), the default tooltip's `GameTooltipDefaultContainer` (`SaftUI-Misc/tooltipposition.lua`; the tooltip itself is never moved) and, on a shaman, Forever's totem bar `MultiCastActionBarFrame` (`SaftUI-Misc/totemposition.lua`, mover `totembar`) all follow the same pattern:
  - Use a holder.
  - Re-anchor the Blizzard frame with `st:SetPointDirect` / `st:ClearAllPointsDirect` in post-hooks on its `SetPoint` / `ClearAllPoints` / `UpdateSystem`. If it's protected (the totem bar holds secure buttons), the re-anchor waits for the end of combat (`st:QueueAfterCombat`), and the holder goes through `st:SafeReposition`. For a frame anchored over its holder by two corners, `st:HoldFrame(frame, holder, x, y)` (`api.lua`; `x` and `y` outside the holder on each side) gives the anchor and its re-anchor post-hooks on `ClearAllPoints` and `SetPoint`, with the wait for the end of combat on a protected frame; the buffs and the swing timers use it. It hooks no `UpdateSystem` and doesn't place the holder: the site still does that.
  - Where Blizzard's Edit Mode can read the frame's anchor (a snap break, when the frame it's snapped to hides, records it into the layout through `UpdateSystemAnchorInfo`, and a layout save keeps it), don't anchor the frame straight to a SaftUI holder. Place it on UIParent at the holder's spot instead, from the holder's rect, and again whenever the holder moves or resizes (the totem bar's `TB:AnchorBar`, run from a post-hook on the holder's `SetPoint`). Otherwise the layout names the holder, and Blizzard anchors the frame to a missing frame wherever the holder doesn't exist. Chat copies its holder's own anchor instead (`CHT:PlaceChatFrame`), so the layout would name the holder's relative frame, UIParent by default.
  - Hide its `Selection` box with `st:HideEditModeSelection(frame)` (`api.lua`, a `HookScript('OnShow', Hide)`; see `.agents/taint.md` rule 5). That keeps the box hidden from its next show; the five sites here also hide it at once with their own `if frame.Selection then frame.Selection:Hide() end` after the call.
  - The holder is kept inside the screen edge margin and the Blizzard frame follows it: anchored to it (buffs, swing timers, the tooltip container, the extra buttons), or placed from its rect (the totem bar, whose `TB:AnchorBar` also runs after `st:UpdateScreenMargin`). Chat copies the holder's anchor rather than its spot, so it adds the holder's `st:GetClampShift` and places ChatFrame1 again after `st:UpdateScreenMargin`. Never clamp the Blizzard frame itself: Blizzard's Edit Mode rewrites its clamp insets on every layout apply. The game still clamps the frame with Blizzard's insets, from its `Selection` box (ChatFrame1's keep it 32 in from the screen's left and bottom, 25 from its right and 60 from its top; the swing timer bars' and the tooltip container's keep them on screen; BuffFrame's let it 15 past the right). Where that clamp holds back the frame the user sees, the holder goes no nearer the edges than the clamp lets the frame go: `st:KeepHolderInScreenMargin(holder, frame, x, y)` (`api.lua`; `x` and `y` how far the frame sits outside the holder) gives the holder that as its reach and applies it again after each of the frame's `UpdateClampOffsets`, reading the frame's clamp and changing only the holder. Chat, the swing timers (their bars sit 5 and 4 outside the holders, so a margin under that would leave a bar behind) and the tooltip call it. Otherwise, where the margin is narrower, the holder and the outline on it go on past a frame its own clamp stopped (chat's outline 12 past its panel at the default margin). The buffs and the totem bar don't: their buttons are anchored to the holder itself, so only an empty Blizzard frame would stay behind, and their holders keep to the margin alone (below 0 they go past the edges, buttons and all).
  - List which Blizzard settings become unreachable, and rebuild the useful ones in `/sui`.
  - Keep first load from moving anything. Profiles saved before the takeover have no position, so either give `position` a default that reproduces Blizzard's default layout (swing timer, buffs) or give it none and capture the frame's spot on screen once Blizzard's Edit Mode has placed it (the totem bar, `TB:CapturePosition`, and chat's size, `CHT:CaptureSize`). Blizzard's layout can arrive after SaftUI loads, and a frame in its default position can sit somewhere temporary until Blizzard lays it out (a totem bar in its default position waits at UIParent's top-left until it first shows), so capture outside Blizzard's re-places, on the next frame.

  Read `.agents/taint.md` first.
- **When post-hooks can't hold it.** The extra action button and the zone ability (`SaftUI-ActionBars/extras.lua`) live in Blizzard's `ExtraAbilityContainer`, which lays its frames out again each time one is added, in combat too. `ExtraActionBarFrame` holds a secure button, so a post-hook couldn't move it back until combat ends. So SaftUI moves none of Blizzard's frames. It anchors what Blizzard never re-anchors (`ExtraActionButton1`, the zone's `SpellButtonContainer` and `Style` art) to the holder once, out of combat, and the buttons then show at SaftUI's spot even when they first appear mid-fight. Blizzard's empty frames stay in Blizzard's layout; make any that take the mouse click-through (`AB:DisableMouse` in `SaftUI-ActionBars/hideblizzard.lua`), so they don't block clicks to what's under them. Before using this, grep both branches to confirm nothing re-anchors the inner frame. The totem bar combines both patterns: Blizzard re-places the bar itself in combat, so its buttons are anchored straight to the holder too and only the empty bar frame moves until combat ends. Blizzard's own button update re-anchors two of them to the bar (the first element and the summon pages), so in a fight that runs it while the bar is elsewhere they follow the bar until combat ends. Size Blizzard's buttons with a Scale setting (the widget's own `SetScale`), since their art is laid out for one size.
- **Validating anchors.** The Position editor's typed relative frame is validated with `st:ValidateAnchor(target, name)`. Always pass `target` (the frame being moved) so self-anchors, anchor loops and descendants are rejected.
- **Profile switches.** `EM:ClearPositions` then `EM:UpdateConfig` re-apply every mover. Implement `UpdateConfig` in your module too (see `.agents/architecture.md`).
- **Test modes.** Loot rolls/feed, party/raid groups and boss frames have session-only test toggles that draw SaftUI-owned previews anchored to the holder (the unit frames' Test mode sits in General's Layout, `SaftUI-Unitframes/config/groupoptions.lua`; their previews are `grouppreview.lua`). Group previews stand in for the header and hide while it shows. Boss frames have no header, so their previews (`UF:UpdateGroupPreview('boss')`) go where `UF:PlaceUnitFrame` puts each boss frame (always one line, Growth and Spacing its only Layout options: `UF.GetUnitGroupLayout` ignores any column key a profile saved), and hide while any real boss frame is shown (`UF:WatchFramesForPreview` hooks their `OnShow`/`OnHide`). Never fake units or override Blizzard APIs.

## Screen edge margin

`misc.clamp_inset` (default `st.CLAMP_INSET`, 20), Screen edge margin on Global Settings' General tab, is how far in from the screen's edges SaftUI keeps the frames the user can move (below 0, how far past them they can go). It's measured by the frame's rect, not its outline or border, in the frame's own units (UIParent's for SaftUI's frames). The defaults sit at least that far in by their rects (the buffs at `-st.CLAMP_INSET`), so first load pushes nothing.
- **How.** `st:KeepInScreenMargin(frame, reach)` (`SaftUI-Core/api.lua`) clamps the frame to the screen with insets of `-m, m, m, -m` (the game keeps its rect, grown by them, on screen) and remembers it. `reach` is optional: a function returning how far the frame's art reaches past its left, right, top and bottom edges, read each time the margin is applied, which grows the insets so the art stays inside the margin and not only the rect. A holder with a Blizzard frame on it uses it for how much further in the frame's own clamp keeps it (`st:KeepHolderInScreenMargin`, above). `st:GetScreenMargin(frame)` is the margin plus the reach on each side, which the game's clamp and edit mode's drags both keep. Calling it again for a frame re-applies the margin with the current reach (`/sui`'s window does, from `Relayout`). `st:UpdateScreenMargin()` re-applies the margin to every frame it remembers: the General tab's row and a profile switch (`Config:UpdateConfig`, before the modules place their frames) run it. Both are blocked in combat on a protected frame, which keeps its margin until combat ends; one queued re-apply (`QueueAfterCombat('ScreenMargin')`) then does them all.
- **Who gets it.** Every mover's frame (`st:RegisterMover`), holders included; every window with a header (`st:CreateHeader`: the bags and bank); `/sui`'s window and dialog; and the holders of the Blizzard frames SaftUI moves, which follow them (see the takeover pattern above). It holds a window's rect, not the metal's title bar above it under the Blizzard theme, where its header (title, buttons and drag handle) sits: at a margin under 20 that can go off the top of the screen (`docs/followups.md`, "Blizzard theme: in-game checks"). `/sui`'s window is the exception: it passes `reach` (`ThemeArt.MetalReach()` under Blizzard), so its metal stays inside the margin and the title bar can't leave the screen.
- **Who doesn't.** Blizzard's frames. The Minimap (its mover sets `blizzardFrame`) keeps to the margin only when dragged or nudged. `st:EnableMoving` only keeps its frames on screen: every caller hands it a Blizzard UI panel (`SettingsPanel`, `AddonList`, `TransmogFrame`, `WorldMapFrame`, `TimeManagerFrame`). ChatFrame1's insets are Blizzard's Edit Mode's; chat follows its holder instead, which goes no nearer the edges than those insets let ChatFrame1 go.
- **Where it shows.** The game shows a frame anchored into the margin at the margin, and leaves its anchor as set: a Position typed in `/sui`, or saved before the margin, stays in the profile. `st:GetClampShift(frame)` is how far the clamp moved it, so a drag or nudge starts from where it shows and stores that; chat adds it to ChatFrame1's copied anchor.

Tests: `tests/core/screen_margin_test.lua` (who gets it, when it's re-applied, combat, Blizzard frames, drags and nudges, `st:GetClampShift`, holders with a Blizzard frame on them), `tests/chat/screen_margin_test.lua` (chat's outline and ChatFrame1 stopping together), `tests/misc/tooltip_margin_test.lua` (the tooltip's holder), `tests/misc/swing_timer_margin_test.lua` (the swing timers' holders), `tests/core/general_tab_test.lua` (the row) and `tests/misc/screen_margin_defaults_test.lua` (the buffs' default).

## `/sui` options

- `/sui` is SaftUI's own window (`st.OptionsWindow`, below), drawn from the tree `Config:GetOptionsTable` builds from each module's `GetConfigTable()`. AceConfigDialog no longer draws it.
- `st.Config:Open(...path)` rebuilds the tree, registers it with AceConfigRegistry as `st.name`, and opens the window at the path's page, picker value and tab (`OM.SelectionForPath`). `st.Config:Close()` closes it, and `st.Config:GetWindow()` is its frame while it's open, else nil. `st.Config:Refresh()` (AceConfigRegistry's NotifyChange) re-renders an open window on the next frame.
- **How a table maps onto the window.** The model (`SaftUI-Core/Config/optionsmodel.lua`, `st.OptionsModel`) reads AceConfig option tables as AceConfig defines them, so a module's `GetConfigTable` is a plain AceConfig table:
  - A top-level group is a sidebar page: its `name` is the page title and its `desc` the line under it.
  - The page's child groups that aren't inline are its tabs (whatever its `childGroups`, except `'select'`, which makes them the picker). Inside the chosen tab or pick, child groups that aren't inline are a second tab row, or the picker when that group says `'select'`. Anything deeper is drawn as sections on the page; nothing is dropped.
  - Inline groups (`inline = true`, or the parent's `childGroups = 'inline'`) are sections titled with their `name`, with any `desc` as a note. A `header` starts a section titled with its name. Options after a nested group continue in an untitled section.
  - Options are rows in `order` (100 by default, negative orders last, then by name), packed by `size`: `'small'` (toggle, execute, input only; a color is never small, its bar being a dropdown's width) takes a quarter of a line, two to a half; no `size` is normal and takes a half; `'wide'`, and every `description` and `position` row, take a line of their own. Ace's `width` is not read. An execute's `align` (SaftUI's field, `'right'` or `'center'`) makes it a button lined up with the aligned buttons next to it, at its own width (see the body's line of buttons, below).
  - Hidden and `guiHidden` options are left out; disabled ones are dimmed and don't respond. A disabled control (but a multiselect, whose rows keep the mouse) takes no mouse, so hovering it shows its row's tooltip, where an option's own `disabledDesc` (SaftUI's: a string, or a function called with `info`; never inherited, read only while it's disabled) says why, under the desc in Text > Red. `get`, `set`, `func`, `validate`, `confirm`, `disabled`, `hidden` and `handler` are inherited from parent groups, method-name strings resolve on the handler, and function-valued `name`, `desc`, `values`, `disabled` and `hidden` are called with an AceConfigDialog-style `info`.
  - A failed `validate` shows in the footer's status line (an input keeps the text typed). `confirm` asks in SaftUI's confirm dialog. A `set` re-renders the page.
  - Each type has a widget: toggle, range (slider and value box), select (a dropdown; `dialogControl = 'LSM30_Font'` lists each font in its own face), multiselect (checkboxes), input, color (a bar as wide and tall as a dropdown, filled with the color and reading its hex code, `#RRGGBB` or `#RRGGBB · 60%` with `hasAlpha` under 100%, always in white, which the font's black outline keeps readable on any color; a click opens Blizzard's color picker), execute (a button), keybinding, description (its text, and AceConfigDialog's image: `image`, `imageCoords`, `imageWidth` and `imageHeight`, the image at the row's left and the text to its right; SaftUI's `justifyH`, `'LEFT'` (the default), `'CENTER'` or `'RIGHT'`, sets the text across the row, and SaftUI's `fill` makes its row a fill row, below, with what it shows in the middle).
  - **A footer control.** A page's top-level group may set `footer` (SaftUI's field, never in its `args`): a group (`type = 'group'`, a `name`, `args`) whose options the window draws in its footer, between Reload UI and Close, while that page is open (`OM.BuildView` hands it on as `view.footer`). Its name is the one label (field font, grey), then each option's widget in order, 8 apart, with the options kit's own widgets (`W.Create` and `Bind`, from a tree of its own, as the picker's), so it takes both looks; it's meant for a select and buttons, and the select's own name isn't drawn. A set draws the page again, as a row's does; `hidden`, `disabled` and the inherited members work as on a page. It's centred in the region; while edit mode's buttons take the region's left (`OW:SetFooterInset`) and centring would bring it closer than 12 to them, it sits at the region's right end beside Close, and when even there it would, without its label (the widgets stay, at the right end, however close; `OW.FooterPlace` in `optionslayout.lua`). The status line ends 12 before it. ## Options window (`SaftUI-Core/Config/optionswindow.lua` and the files beside it)

`st.OptionsWindow` is five files, which `SaftUI-Core/Config/load.xml` loads in this order, after `optionsbody.lua` and before `generators.lua`: `optionslayout.lua` (`OW.LAYOUT`, `OW.SidebarWidth`, `OW.SidebarEntries`, `OW.LayoutRows` and `OW.LayoutSections`, the window's height: `OW.MaxHeight`, `OW.WindowHeight`, and where a page's footer control sits: `OW.FooterPlace`; it loads first, so it makes `OW` and `OW.S`), `optionswindow.lua` (the state, the saved position, scrolling, re-rendering, search, the body and the pages, `Relayout` and `Draw`, building the frame, the footer, the keyboard and the API), `optionsresize.lua` (growing taller: the grip, the bottom edge, the resize, `ApplyHeight`), `optionsnav.lua` (the sidebar, the tabs and the picker) and `optionssmalldialog.lua` (the small dialog, with `OW:OpenDialog`, `OW:CloseDialog`, `OW:IsDialogShown` and `OW.GetDialogFrames`). What they share with each other and nothing else is `OW.S` (not an API). The state more than one of them reads or writes that is replaced as it changes (`S.layout`, `S.blizzard`, `S.shown`, `S.sizing`, `S.scrollOffset`, `S.navOffset`, `S.navHeight`, `S.filledTo`) is always read and written as `S.name`, never kept in a local, so no file holds a stale copy. Anything else (a constant, a table that's only filled such as `S.F`, `S.state` or `S.pools`, a function) a file sets on `S` at its end and a later file takes as a local of its own at its top; what `OB` exports (`OB.Text`, `OB.ScrollTo`, `OB.ROW_HEIGHT`) a file takes from `OB`. A file takes only from files before it, so keep that order when adding to them; `optionswindow.lua` calls the later files' functions (`S.DrawSidebar`, `S.DrawTabRow`, `S.BuildResize`, ...) through `S` when they run. Tests load the files with `load_options_window()` (`tests/lib/shell.lua`), whose list `tests/lib/harness_self_test.lua` checks against `load.xml`.

`st.OptionsWindow` (`OW`) draws `/sui` as the approved mockup lays it out (`docs/superpowers/specs/assets/2026-11-09-options-window-mockup.html`): drawn as a `window` in SaftUI's theme with a Solid background (so it never turns see-through), at `FULLSCREEN_DIALOG` level 100, so edit mode's outlines draw under it. It has two layouts, `OW.LAYOUT.own` (the mockup's, under Thick and Thin: 1040 wide, at least 700 tall) and `OW.LAYOUT.blizzard` (Blizzard's Settings window under Blizzard: 1110 wide, at least 728 tall), chosen on each render by `W.BlizzardLook()` and applied only when the look changed (`Relayout`; the page, tab and scroll are kept). Inside the main column nothing moves between them. Under Blizzard the window is the metal (`window` kind) with a title bar ("SaftUI Options" in the window-title font, centred on the title strip, which is also the drag strip), the close X in the metal's socket (`st.ThemeArt.Close()`), `Options_InnerFrame` stretched over the sidebar and the main column (17 in from the metal's sides, 28 down, ending above the footer), and the footer across the window under it with Reload UI and Close 16 in from the window's bottom corners; the brand is hidden and the search box, with Edit mode's buttons under it, sits at the sidebar's top. It and its dialog are kept inside the screen edge margin (`/sui` also keeps its metal in, `st:KeepInScreenMargin`'s `reach`). The model (`optionsmodel.lua`, `st.OptionsModel`) turns the tree into pages, tabs, a picker, sections and rows; the window draws them:
- **Sidebar** (210 wide under Thick and Thin, 242 under Blizzard, `OW.SidebarWidth`: its right edge is on the divider drawn into the inner frame): the search box (`F.search`, 12 in and 10 down in the sidebar's actions, as wide as the line of two command buttons under it and 8 above them, in both layouts; under Blizzard `SearchBoxTemplate`'s art and magnifying glass, its text and hint starting clear of the glass, `W.LookSearch`), Edit mode and the command buttons under it (`DrawActions`, `optionsnav.lua`), then Global Settings, Profiles and the category groups (`OW.SidebarEntries`), and the profile's name at the bottom. While SaftUI's theme is Blizzard the entries are the Settings panel's category list: the open page on `Options_List_Active`, the one under the mouse on `Options_List_Hover`, in place of the blue fill and bar, their names gold (white for the open page and under the mouse), and the three group headers (FRAMES, COMBAT, INFO) are the Settings banners (`OW.BannerAtlas(n)`, `Options_CategoryHeader_1` to `_3`, their art hanging below the header's text without covering the first entry). The sidebar's own fill and lines (`F.sideFill`, `F.sideLines`, in `OW.GetFrames`) and the footer's top line (`F.footerLine`) show only under Thick and Thin. When the entries overflow the sidebar scrolls (`OB.ScrollTo`, narrow Blizzard bar, none under Thick and Thin).
- **Header:** the page's name (Colus 34) and desc (15, grey), and the close X (`st:CreateCloseButton`). Dragging the header, the sidebar's title (Thick and Thin) or the title bar (Blizzard) moves the window; where it's left is saved as `st.config.global.optionsWindow = { point, x, y, height }` (account-wide, never in a profile) and restored on open. A move keeps the height that was saved, and a window never moved or resized saves nothing.
- **Growing taller** (every theme). The window is dragged taller by its grip (`F.grip`, 16 x 16 at the bottom right corner: Blizzard's `PanelResizeButtonTemplate` art under Blizzard, `Control('resizegrip')`, which the metal's corner leaves room for and which stays clear of Close; SaftUI's corner arrow, as `st:EnableResizing` draws it, under Thick and Thin) or by its bottom edge (`F.sizer`, a 6 tall strip left of the grip). Its top stays; the height is made even (the window is centred, so its edges stay on whole pixels), at least the layout's `height` and at most `OW.MaxHeight(L)` (the screen less the screen edge margin at the top and at the bottom, the margin counting as 0 below 0 so it never outgrows the screen, and under Blizzard less the metal's reach above and below), and a drag also stops where the window's bottom, or the metal under it, meets the margin, so it never pushes the window up. `OW.WindowHeight(L)` gives the height on open, on a relayout and on a change of the screen edge margin while it shows (`st:UpdateScreenMargin` is hooked in `Build`, as chat and the totem bar hook it, to `ApplyHeight`): the saved number made even and held to those limits, the layout's least with none saved. Only a resize that ended at a different height than it began writes `optionsWindow.height`, so a click on the grip saves nothing. A resize renders nothing while the button is held (`Busy` counts it); each change re-places the body's and the nav's scroll bars (`FollowHeight`: `SetScroll`, `SetNavScroll`), except the body's on a page with a fill row, which draws its fill row once, when the mouse is let go, and places the body's bar then (re-placed mid-drag over a row drawn to the old height, it would show over the page's own bars). The window hiding mid-resize ends it and saves the height. There is no resize cursor on the handles (`SetCursor` isn't stubbed; see `docs/followups.md`).
- **Tabs** (17, the active one underlined blue, a disabled one faded to 0.4 in its place, taking no click and not lit on hover, in every theme; a disabled tab the selection names opens only while every tab beside it is disabled, and Search leaves out the tabs it can't open; under Blizzard the Settings panel's tabs, `Options_Tab_*`, the active one picked, `Options_Tab_Active_*`, in place of the underline), wrapping to more lines when they don't fit the content's width (774; no room is kept for the search box, which is the sidebar's); a page's single tab isn't drawn. A second row for subtabs, then the picker row (the group's `pickerLabel`, else its name, and a select widget). Under Blizzard the tab row's line (and the subtab row's) is Blizzard's horizontal divider (`Options_HorizontalDivider`) in place of the 1px line.
- **Body:** a scroll frame of sections, drawn by a body object (`st.OptionsBody`, `optionsbody.lua`: `OB.New(content, root, onChanged, width)`, then `body:Draw(sections)` or `body:DrawResults(sections, onPick)`, `body:Refresh(except)` mid-drag (see Re-rendering), and `body:Release()`). Sections (`OB.LayoutSections`) have a 1px `#2e2e2e` line and a Colus 15 title between them, never boxes; under Blizzard there is no line between sections (every options body: the window, a dialog, the Categories editor), the layout unchanged. The vertical dividers between halves stay. An untitled section after another (rows that follow a nested group) gets no line, so it reads as a continuation. Rows (`OB.LayoutRows`) are 34 high in two 367-wide columns 40 apart, each two quarters at least 24 apart (`QUARTER_GAP` in `optionsbody.lua`); a wide row takes a line of its own. Every cell starts and ends on a whole pixel (`body:CellRect(col, quarter)`): halves and quarters round down, and the second of each pair sits flush with the outer edge, so a 367 column's quarters are 171 wide and 25 apart. A 1px border on a frame between two pixels blurs, since SaftUI's scale makes a unit a pixel; for the same reason the select's caret rows are placed by their left edge. The label (16) is on the left and the widget right-aligned, check boxes and swatches too. ## Options window widgets (`SaftUI-Core/Config/optionswidgets.lua` and the files after it)

`st.OptionsWidgets` is ten files, which `SaftUI-Core/Config/load.xml` loads in this order, after `optionsmodel.lua` and before `optionsbody.lua`: `optionswidgets.lua` (the registry: `W` itself, `W.WIDTH`, the base widget, the pools, `W.Create`, `define` and `W.Define`), `optionslook.lua` (the look the controls share: the field font, `Box` and its edges, each control's Blizzard art, the check box), `optionsdialog.lua` (`W.Confirm`, `W.Prompt`), `optionsbar.lua` (`W.BlizzardBar`), `optionslist.lua` (the select list and the check list), `optionstypes.lua` (toggle, input, color, execute, description), `optionsrange.lua`, `optionsselect.lua` (select, multiselect), `optionskeybinding.lua` and `optionsposition.lua`. What they share with each other and nothing else is `W.Kit`: each file ends by setting there the locals a later file needs, and starts by taking what it needs as locals of its own (what `W` exports, such as `W.Box` or `W.LookDropdown`, it takes from `W`). A file takes only from files before it, so keep that order when adding to them. `optionsposition.lua` is the last type file: it ends by recording the built-in types, the ones `W.Define` refuses to replace, so a new type's file goes before it. Tests load the files with `load_options_widgets()` (`tests/lib/shell.lua`), whose list `tests/lib/harness_self_test.lua` checks against `load.xml`.

`st.OptionsWidgets` (`W`) draws the controls of SaftUI's own options window. `W.Create(parent, rowType)` gives a pooled widget (toggle, range, select, multiselect, input, color, execute, keybinding, description, position). `position` is the Position block: dots for the point and relative point, a frame name box or a dropdown, and X over Y, each with a 14x22 nudge box (up and down are separate buttons) sharing the edit box's right seam; its `get`/`set`/`validate` take the part first (`point`, `rel_point`, `x_off`, `y_off`, `frame`, `relative_to`). The range's track is textures of its slider, under the knob by draw layer: a child frame given a lower frame level drew over the knob in game once the widget was reparented (reparenting and level changes move children back above their parent). `widget:Bind(root, row, onChanged)` reads with `OM.Get`, and a change the user makes runs `OM.Validate`, then `OM.Confirm` (asked with `W.Confirm`), then `OM.Set`, and calls `onChanged(ok, err)` (with `true` and the widget after them while a slider is dragged). `widget:Refresh()` reads the row again. `widget:Release()` returns it to the pool. A description draws its `image` (a texture path or fileID, or a function giving that with a width and height; else `imageWidth` and `imageHeight`, 32 each by default) at its left, cropped by `imageCoords` (4 or 8 numbers, or a function giving them; any other count shows the whole texture, as AceGUI's label), with its text to the right, in the image's middle when the image is taller, and it's as tall as the taller of the two. Anything but a path or fileID draws no image. The image has a border in SaftUI's theme, drawn like an action button's icon (`'theme'`, `'theme'`, kind `action`), shown only with an image: Icon zoom's preview is one. The border lies outside the image, so `Fit` places the image the border's width (`st:ThemeInset('theme', 'theme', 'action')`) in from the row's top-left and counts the border in the text's left and the row's height: the row's edge is the scroll area's, which cuts what's drawn past it. A theme picked on the Theme tab reaches the row when it's drawn again. The select list and the confirm dialog are at `TOOLTIP` strata, above the window. `W.Prompt(text, initial, onAccept, opts)` is the same dialog with an edit box (one modal at a time: opening a confirm or prompt answers the open one No); it replaces StaticPopups, which sit under the window. UF's profile New, Copy, Rename, Delete, Export and Import use it (`SaftUI-Unitframes/config/profiles.lua`). `W.OpenCheckList(owner, entries, isChecked, onToggle)` opens the select list's frames as a list of check boxes (`entries` are `{ key, label }`, each row marked by `isChecked(key)`): a click on a row calls `onToggle(key)` and draws the marks again, leaving the list open; a click outside, Esc and `W.CloseList` close it, and `W.ListOwner()` reports its owner, so the window waits to re-render while it's open. **Custom types.** `W.Define(rowType, { width, bare, titled, build = function(w) end, methods = { Refresh, OnRelease, ... } })` adds a widget type through the same machinery as the built-in ones: `build` makes `w.frame` once per widget, the methods are copied onto each widget (a `Refresh` after a release does nothing), and `W.Create`, `Bind`, `Commit`, `SetDisabled` and `Release` work as for the others. Its look follows SaftUI's theme: under Blizzard it is Blizzard's red close button (its four textures at 28 on Forever, 24 on retail, `st.ThemeArt.Close()`), switched on every recolor and redraw (`st:KeepOwnColors`). On a window drawn in the metal (`st:CloseButtonInSocket`) it moves into the metal's socket at TOPRIGHT of the window's drawn frame, and back to the header's right under Thick and Thin; a module that draws its window again with its own Theme calls `st:UpdateCloseButton(frame)` (the bags). A button in no header (`/sui`'s window and dialog, an item tooltip) keeps the place its caller gave it. The window's header goes with it, close button or none: into the metal's title bar (`st:HeaderInTitleBar`), the title bar's strip (`st.ThemeArt.TitleStrip()`, 20 on both clients) with its bottom on the window's top, so its title, its buttons and what the window places under it move up with it, and back to the window's top under Thick and Thin. Every recolor and redraw places it (`st:KeepOwnColors`), and `st:UpdateHeader(header)` does too, which a module that draws its window with its own Theme calls after `st:UpdateCloseButton` (the bags). A button in a header is `st:HeaderButtonSize(frame)` tall (the header less 8, or the strip less 4, 16, in the title bar), stops `st:SocketReach(frame)` in from the header's right edge while the close button is in the socket (26 on Forever, 21 on retail), and a window sized around its content counts `st:HeaderRoom(frame)` (none in the title bar) rather than the header's height. What a window places above its header clears the art by `st:ArtAboveHeader(frame)`: under the metal, its top (16 over the drawn frame, `st.ThemeArt.MetalAbove()`) less how high the header sits; the bags' bag slot bars sit on the header's top, `buttonspacing` and that up (`INV:PlaceBagSlotBar`). A header or footer that's protected in combat (a bag window's: Blizzard's item buttons hang from the scroll frame anchored under its header, and the search box anchored to it is its footer's child) isn't moved or sized until combat ends: `st:UpdateHeader`, which a profile switch and Title bar height run at once even in combat, leaves it and its title-bar state as they are and queues one `st:UpdateHeader()` for after combat (`QueueAfterCombat('UpdateHeaders')`).

## Ace3's own windows

SaftUI doesn't use AceConfigDialog or AceGUI: `/sui`, its dialog and every option widget are SaftUI's. Core's TOCs still load both (with the LibSharedMedia AceGUI widgets) as libraries for other addons. SaftUI's reskin of them (`aceskin.lua`) is gone; it only ever styled SaftUI's own windows, so other addons' Ace windows look as they did. Don't hook `AceGUI:Create`, a widget constructor or `AceGUI:Release`: AceGUI's widgets are pooled and shared with every addon.
