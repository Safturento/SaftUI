# Edit mode and `/sui`

SaftUI has its own edit mode. It never adds frames to Blizzard's Edit Mode.
- `/sui edit` or the `/sui` "Edit mode" button toggles it. It also follows Blizzard's Edit Mode opening and closing.
- Registered frames get a draggable overlay. Arrow keys nudge 1px, Shift+arrows 10px.
- Where overlays overlap, a list by the cursor names every one under it and marks the one a click or drag gets; the rest dim. Alt brings the next one to the front (`EditMode/picker.lua`, like `/fstack`). Overlays share one strata, so their frame levels decide which is on top: each gets its own pair (backdrop one below), handed out rising by `EM:BringToFront`. Don't set an overlay's level anywhere else.
- Clicking a frame opens `/sui` at that frame's section.
- A grid shows behind the UI while edit mode is on and Grid is enabled (`EditMode/grid.lua`). Two center lines sit on UIParent's center, rounded to a physical pixel (`EM:GetGridCenter`), and the other lines repeat outward every Grid size (`EM.GridLinePositions`), each 1 physical pixel thick. Snapping reads the same two functions, so what's drawn is what snaps. The grid also redraws from its own frame's `OnSizeChanged` (the screen events can fire before UIParent resizes), on the screen events, and on `st.UIScale:OnChanged` (SaftUI's own `SetScale` fires no screen event). The lines are pooled textures on a SaftUI frame at `BACKGROUND` strata that never takes the mouse. **Dim world** (also `grid.lua`) darkens the world under it: `EM.dimFrame` is a SaftUI frame on all of UIParent at `BACKGROUND` strata, one level under `EM.gridFrame`, taking no mouse, with one black texture at `EM.DIM_ALPHA` (0.5). `EM:UpdateDim()` shows it while edit mode and the `dim` setting (on by default) are both on; it runs on `Enabled`, `Disabled` and `SettingsChanged`.
- **Coordinates** (`EditMode/coordinates.lua`). With the bar's Coordinates on (`coordinates`, off by default), the selected mover's position shows as a small label outside its outline, at the corner its position is anchored by, in words and naming the parent: "Top left of the screen: 120, -330", "Bottom to top of Bar 2: 0, 3" (`EM.CoordinatesText(position, nameOf)`: the stored position, offsets read as numbers; the parent is the screen for no frame, `UIParent` or UIParent, else `EM.MoverNameOf(ref)` (the `EM.MoverName` of the mover whose frame it is) or the stored name). During a drag it follows the dragged mover's live `drag.x`/`drag.y` (an `OnUpdate` on `EM.coordinates`), selected or not. It is placed again on `UI_SCALE_CHANGED`, `DISPLAY_SIZE_CHANGED` and `st.UIScale:OnChanged` (one named function; SaftUI's own scale change fires no screen event). `EM.CoordinatesSpot(point, rect, width, height, screenWidth, screenTop, avoid)` places it, whole, outside the outline at the mover's own point (a LEFT point aligns left edges, a RIGHT point right edges, a TOP point puts it above, a BOTTOM point below; a plain LEFT or RIGHT puts it beside it), `EM.COORD_GAP` (2) away; where that leaves the screen or goes under the bar (`avoid`, the docked bar's `EM:BarRect()`: where the label is across the rect's width the top it may reach is the rect's bottom, else the screen's; a label beside the bar can sit in the strip) that axis flips inside the outline, and the result is clamped on screen. `EM.coordinates` (`.text`) is a SaftUI frame at `FULLSCREEN_DIALOG` level 9600 (over the overlays, the bar and the floating window, under the History list), taking no mouse, in the pixel font and the accent (`st:Color('accent')`, Theme > Accent). It hides with Coordinates off, nothing selected or dragged, outside edit mode, in combat, and when `EM.GetOutlineRect` gives nil (a secret edge). While shown it has an `OnUpdate` that looks again every 0.1 s (every frame in a drag), because a position changed in `/sui` (the Position block's setters, a reset) fires no edit mode event; it fires no `PositionChanged` itself, and a hidden label has no `OnUpdate`. Its size is `ceil` of the text's, as the kit sizes a label. The label also re-places itself on the screen events. `EM.bar` is the bar's frame once built (nil before). The label reads `EM:BarRect()` (nil while the bar floats, is hidden or is hidden until hovered) and re-places itself on `BarModeChanged`.
- **What shows** (`EditMode/filter.lua`; the bar's controls for it are `Bar/barshow.lua`). One rule, `EM:IsMoverShown(mover)`: a mover outside the module choice (`hidden_modules`, `[module] = true` for an unticked one) or the filter (`EM.filterText`, lowercase, not saved, cleared on `Disabled`; a plain, case-ignored find in `EM.MoverName`) never shows; else a mover whose frame is turned off (`EM:IsMoverDisabled`) shows under **Show disabled** (`show_disabled`, off by default), one whose frame is on but not on screen now (`EM:IsMoverHidden`) under **Show hidden** (`show_hidden`, ON by default: the target, focus, pet, party, raid, boss, closed bags and loot rolls are what edit mode places, and would otherwise be missing until a button is pressed), and every other shows. The two toggles are separate; the choices persist in the profile. `EM:UpdateShown()` sets every overlay to `EM.active and EM:IsMoverShown(mover)` (a mover being dragged stays), deselects a selected mover that no longer shows (its selection and Coordinates label go; `/sui` stays on the page it shows, as after Esc), updates overlaps and labels, and fires `ShownChanged` (no arguments) only when an overlay was shown or hidden, a selection was dropped or a mover's overlap state changed, since the ticker runs it twice a second. A predicate that errors is reported once per mover for a session of edit mode (`EditMode/editmode.lua`'s `RunPredicate`, cleared on `Disabled`), and counts as false. It runs on `Activate`, on `SettingsChanged`, when the filter changes and every `EM.SHOWN_INTERVAL` (0.5) seconds from `EM.shownFrame` while edit mode is on, because the hidden checks read live frame state (a bag opens, a pet comes). Showing or hiding an overlay never touches the mover's own frame, which may be protected. `EM:CreateMover` gives a mover made while edit mode is on its overlay by the same rule. `EM:GetModules()` is the modules with a mover, A to Z, `EM.OTHER_MODULE` last; `EM:SetModuleShown`, `EM:SetAllModulesShown` write `hidden_modules` through `EM:SetSetting` (a ticked module's key is cleared, never false); `EM.ShowFaceText(modules, isShown)` is the Show list's face ("All modules", the name, "3 modules", "Nothing"). `EM:SelectFirstMatch()` selects the first shown mover by name. **Labels where movers overlap** (design decision 25): `mover.overlaps` is true when the mover's frame rect (`EM.GetRectInUI` of `def.frame`, not the outline, which reaches outside the frame) overlaps another shown mover's by more than `EM.OVERLAP_TOLERANCE` (1 UIParent unit) on both axes (`EM.RectsOverlap(a, b, tolerance)`): frames that touch or sit a few pixels apart keep their labels, and a read-back hair is no overlap, recomputed in `UpdateShown` and on `PositionChanged`; `EM:UpdateLabels(hovered)` shows a mover's label unless it overlaps, and then only while it is `hovered`, the picker's top mover under the cursor, or being dragged (a dragged mover keeps its overlay too until the drop, then the rule applies) (`picker.lua`'s `Refresh` calls it when that changes). `EM.GetPickerState()` returns `{ stack, list, driver }` for tests. The bar's controls: group `show` (left, 30, row 2, section `show`: `showDisabled` and `showHidden`, icon toggles; `modules`, the select face (150 wide, 140 floating) opening a check list with a row per module and the buttons All and None, a click applying at once; `filter`, a 150 wide box (140 floating), never dropped, so nothing clears a typed filter behind its back; there is no "Show" caption: the floating window's section has its own title), with the Lucide glass, the hint "Filter movers...", Enter selecting the first match and Esc clearing; it drops focus on `Pressed`, `DragStarted` and `Disabled` so the arrows still nudge; both it and the Show face have tooltips). `EM.keyCatcher` is the key catcher frame (for tests). `EM.BarGuard` and `EM.BarToggle` (`bargroups.lua`) are the click guard and the toggle helper `barshow.lua` reuses.
- **The bar.** Edit mode's controls are on a small two-row bar on the top edge of the screen (`EditMode/Bar/`; `/sui`'s footer no longer holds any). `bar.lua` is the frame, the group list, the layout and the events; `bargroups.lua` is the Exit, grid and options groups, `barshow.lua` the Show and filter groups, `barchanges.lua` the changes group and the status line, `historylist.lua` the History list, `bardock.lua` where the docked bar sits, slides and hides, and what it covers. A SaftUI frame in `FULLSCREEN_DIALOG` at level `EM.BAR.LEVEL` (9500: over every mover, which climb to 9000 (`picker.lua`), and over `/sui` at 100; under everything in `TOOLTIP` strata), `EM.BAR.HEIGHT` (70: `VPAD` 7, a row of `ROW` 26, `ROW_GAP` 4, a row, 7) tall and only as wide as its wider row (see Layout), drawn as a panel in the theme on the options Background (`st:SetBackdrop(bar, 'theme', 'options', 'panel')`: see-through with the theme's Background unless Global settings > Theme > Options windows says Solid, see Themes in `.agents/architecture.md`): its backdrop sits the theme's inset outside the frame. The frame takes the mouse: its blank parts are the handle that slides it (below), and a click there selects no mover under it (a control takes its own); beside the bar the movers and the Alt picker are as ever. It shows on `Enabled` (`EM:GetBar` builds it on first use) and hides on `Disabled`, with `/sui` open or closed, and it hides in combat, as edit mode does (every control's click also does nothing in combat). On `DragStarted` it fades to `EM.BAR.DRAG_ALPHA` and every control takes no mouse (kept, and given back on `DragStopped`, which `EM:StopDrag` fires even when the commit errors; the bar also resets the fade and the mouse on `Enabled` and `Disabled`), so a frame dragged up under it shows through. The Grid size box lets go of the keyboard on `Pressed`, `DragStarted` and `Disabled`, so the arrow keys keep nudging.
  - **Groups and controls.** `EM:AddBarGroup({ key, order, side = 'left' | 'right', row = 1 | 2, section = 'title' | 'grid' | 'show' | 'changes', controls = function(parent) -> { control, ... } })`, called at file load; `row` is the docked bar's (row 1 when left and row 2 when right if not given; within a row groups run left to right by `order`), `section` is the floating window's. A control is `{ key, frame, Lay, Refresh, Look, section, align }`: `Lay(control, floating) -> width` lays it out and says its width (0 is not shown), `Refresh` puts the settings' state on it, `Look(control, blizzard)` its look (`W.LookControl(frame)` when it has none), `section` overrides the group's. The groups: row 1 is `exit` (10, `title`: `exit`), `grid` (20, `grid`: `grid`, `gridSize`, `snapGrid`, `snapFrames`), `view` (25, section `grid` too: `dim`, `coordinates`: a group of its own so a divider follows the snaps, as in the owner's mockup) and `changes` (80, `changes`: `undo`, `redo`, `history`, `reset`; `barchanges.lua`, with `dockKeys = { undo, redo, reset, history }`: a group's `dockKeys` is the order of its controls on the docked bar only, the mockup's, while the floating window keeps the group's own); row 2 is `show` (30, `show`: `showDisabled`, `showHidden`, `modules`, `filter`; `barshow.lua`) and `options` (90, `changes`: `options`, `hide` (the Hide until hovered toggle, see Where it sits), then `detach`, `section = 'title'`, 26, at the docked group's end; `hide` and `detach` are hidden when floating). The floating window's order is History then Reset; the docked bar's is Reset then History. `EM:BarControl(key)` finds one, `EM:BarControls()` lists them in group order. A control's click is `control.onClick` (never `SetScript('OnClick')`: the kit's controls guard a disabled one in their own script), wrapped in `Guard` (nothing in combat). Controls are children of the bar one level above it (`Raise`: each is made a level up, so their boxes' backdrops stay over the bar's own), centred in their 26 tall row on whole pixels (row 1's top is 7 down from the bar's, row 2's 37; a control 26 tall is at its row's top, 24 one down, 22 two), tooltips under them (`W.ShowTip`).
  - **Layout.** `EM.BarLayout(rows) -> xs, dividers, width` is pure (`rows = { { { widths = { w, ... } }, ... }, ... }`: a row is a list of groups, a group its controls' widths; a width of 0 is skipped, and a group with none whole): each row runs its controls `GAP` (4) apart, with `GROUP_PAD` (8), a 1px divider and `GROUP_PAD` between groups; the bar's `width` is the wider row plus `PAD` (10) at each end, made even, and each row is centred in it (`floor((width - the row's width) / 2)`); `xs[r][g][c]` and `dividers[r]` are whole x from the bar's left. `EM:LayoutBar()` lays every control out with `Lay(control, false)`, sizes the bar (`BAR.HEIGHT` by that width), places the controls by their top left and the dividers (1 x 26 `OB.HAIRLINE` lines, one on a row's group boundary, kept under Blizzard), then `EM:PlaceBar()` and fires `BarLaid`. It lays out on `UI_SCALE_CHANGED` and `DISPLAY_SIZE_CHANGED` (the bar's own frame's events) and on `st.UIScale:OnChanged` (one named function, `OnScreenChanged`, for both; SaftUI's own scale change fires no screen event); `EM:PlaceBar()` again on the next frame, as the events can fire before UIParent has its new size. The two rows are about 560 wide, and fit a 1024 wide screen at every control's full width, so nothing gives way (the old width stages, the Show caption and `EM:BarWidthAt` and `EM:BarGap` are gone). `EM:RefreshBar()` puts each control's look (`W.BlizzardLook()`) and state on again, on `SettingsChanged` and `ColorsChanged`.
  - **Where it sits, sliding and hiding** (`Bar/bardock.lua`). `EM:PlaceBar()` anchors the bar's TOPLEFT to UIParent's at `x` and 0 (against the top edge): the saved `editModeBar.dockX` (account-wide beside `floating`, `x`, `y`; whole; a value that is not a number counts as none), else the middle (`floor((UIParent width - bar width) / 2)`), put inside the screen edge margin (`st:GetScreenMargin`; a saved place the screen no longer has is kept as saved and the bar shown inside). It runs on every layout and when `st:UpdateScreenMargin` runs. **Sliding:** a drag by any blank part of the bar (`RegisterForDrag('LeftButton')`; a control takes its own mouse, so a drag that starts on one slides nothing) moves it by the cursor's travel along the top only, from an `OnUpdate` that runs only during the drag, whole, inside the margin; it ends on `OnDragStop`, on an `OnUpdate` that finds the left button up (a missed mouse-up) and when the bar hides (combat, Exit), and each saves the place (`EM:SetBarX(x)`); none starts in combat, while floating or while a mover is dragged (the bar lets go of the mouse then, `bar.lua`). Two left mouse-ups within 0.3 s on the blank part, neither the end of a drag, return it to the middle and clear the saved place (no tooltip says so). **What it covers:** `EM:BarRect()` is `{ left, right, bottom, top }` in UIParent's units (from its bottom left) of the frame and the line its backdrop draws (the theme's inset), flush with the screen's top, nil while the bar floats, is hidden, is not built or Hide until hovered is on; `EM:BarCoverHeight()` is that rect's height or 0 (a caller that only needs a height reads it). `BarModeChanged` fires whenever the placed x or width changes and when Hide until hovered or the mode changes. `/sui` is offset down only where its rect is under the bar's horizontally (`S.BarCover(right)` in `optionswindow.lua`, see the offset under /sui below), the Coordinates label avoids the rect, and a mover can be dragged into the strip beside the bar (nothing clamps movers by the bar). **Hide until hovered** is the `hide` control, an icon toggle before Detach (`st.icons.squareMousePointer` off and `squareDashedMousePointer` on, drawn on in the accent by `W.SetOn`; tooltip "Hide until hovered", "Hides this bar until the mouse reaches the top edge of the screen where it sits."; not in the floating window), saved as `editModeBar.hide` (default off; `EM:SetBarHideUntilHovered(on)`, `EM:IsBarHideUntilHovered()`). While on (edit mode on, no combat, docked) the bar is hidden with `bar:Hide()` (no animation; `EM:IsBarAway()` is what `ShowSurface` reads) and `EM.barStrip`, a frame as wide as the bar and `BAR.HOT` (6) tall at the top edge where it sits (it moves with the bar), shows its `line`, 2 px (`BAR.STRIP`) in the accent (Theme > Accent). It takes no mouse, so it takes no click from a mover under it; `EM.barWatch`'s `OnUpdate` (shown only while the choice is active) reads the mouse with `IsMouseOver`: the mouse over the strip's rect brings the bar down at once (`Down`: the bar shown, the line hidden), and it stays while the mouse is over the bar, while a list of its own is open (`W.ListOwner()` is one of its controls, or the History list is shown), while one of its boxes has the keyboard, while it is slid, and for `BAR.GRACE` (0.3) s after the last of these (the grace restarts each time); it goes up (`Up`) after that. It never comes down while a mover is dragged (`DragStarted` to `DragStopped`) or pressed with the left button held (`Pressed`, cleared when the button is up), and goes up at once when either begins. Turning it on while the mouse is over the bar leaves the bar down until the mouse leaves; turning it off shows the bar for good. While on the bar covers nothing, hidden or down (`BarRect` nil, cover 0): `/sui` never jumps as the bar comes and goes, and the bar draws over it when down (it is above `/sui` already). Combat and Exit hide the bar and the strip, and edit mode opens in the same state (hidden, strip shown; `UpdateBarDock`).
  - **Events** (`EM:On(event, fn)`): `PositionChanged` (mover), `Selected` (mover or nil), `Enabled`, `Disabled`, `SettingsChanged` (key, or nil for every setting: a profile change), `HistoryChanged` (no arguments: edit mode's history changed, see History below), `Pressed` (mover: the mouse went down on its outline), `DragStarted` and `DragStopped` (mover: `EM:StartDrag`, and `EM:StopDrag` or a drag `EM:UpdateConfig` dropped), `ColorsChanged` (group, key: `EM:OnColorsChanged`, fired only while edit mode is on and for every group: a listener filters by the groups it draws and only colors; while edit mode is off nothing is fired and each part looks again as it comes on, `Enabled`. The outlines recolor for `theme`, and the selected mover for the Accent. The bar, its window and the History list: `EM.DrawsColor(group, key)`, Text's Normal and Muted and the Accent, through `EM:RecolorBar()` (each control's `Recolor(control, blizzard)`, or its `Look`, or `W.LookControl`) and the list's own recolor, with no placing, refresh, `Show` or `Hide`; a Theme pass keeps `EM:RefreshBar()` and the list's look. The docked bar's strip and the Coordinates label take the Accent, the status line Muted, and the grid's two centre lines the Accent at `CENTER_ALPHA` with nothing redrawn. A number box under the mouse takes its rest look from a pass, the edge lit only for the keyboard and the arrows Muted, until the mouse leaves (`EM.RecolorNumberBox` does not ask the mouse); a Theme pass still does the full look, a followup), `BarLaid` (no arguments: the bar or the floating window laid its controls out), `BarModeChanged` (no arguments: what the bar covers changed: it detached or docked, slid, changed width or turned Hide until hovered on or off). `EM.MoverName(mover)` is a label's first line, else the key (the picker's list and the Options tooltip use it).
  - **The floating window** (`Bar/barwindow.lua`). Detach turns the bar into a small window and Dock (in its title) turns it back: `EM:SetBarFloating(floating)` (`EM:IsBarFloating()`) saves the mode in `st.config.global.editModeBar = { floating, x, y }` (account-wide, whole offsets of the window's top left from UIParent's top left; written on a mode change, with the default place when none is saved, and on a drag), closes the History list, the kit's list when a bar control owns it and every focused box, moves every control with `SetParent` (`Adopt` in `bar.lua`; `SetParent` resets a frame's level, so each control's level is set again to two over its parent's and each descendant's to its offset from the control, from a shape taken once when the control was built (`EM.LevelShape`, `EM.SettleLevel`), the same however often it moves), lays out, shows the one that holds them while edit mode is on (neither while it is off, or in combat) and fires `BarModeChanged`. `Enabled` applies the saved mode, so a reload brings the window back where it was left. `EM.WINDOW` (`WIDTH` 312, `PAD` 12, `TITLE` 42, `CAPTION` 13, `CAPTION_GAP` 4, `SECTION_PAD` 8, `GAP` 4) and `EM.WINDOW_SECTIONS` (`grid` "Grid and snap", `show` "Show", `changes` "Changes"; a control's `section` says which, `title` ones, Exit, go in the title row beside Dock). `EM.WindowLayout(width, sections) -> rows, height` is pure (`sections = { { widths, aligns }, ... }`; `rows[s][c] = { x, y }` with y the line's top, `rows[s].top/.caption/.bottom/.rule`): controls wrap into lines `GAP` apart inside `width - 2 * PAD`, a control 100 wide or more starts a new line when the line already holds a narrower one (one way only: a narrow one after it may share; the Show section is its two toggles, then the face and the filter on the next line), a right-aligned one (`align`) ends its line at the right padding, a 0 width is skipped, a section with no control has no caption or line, and a 1px `OB.LINE` runs between sections. `EM:LayoutWindow()` (the floating `LayoutBar`) lays each control out with `Lay(control, true)`, places it by its top left centred in its 26 line, places the captions (`OB.Font('caption')`, Text > Muted), lines, the title (`OB.Font('section')`) and Dock. `EM:GetBarWindow()` (`EM.barWindow`; `.dock`, `.title`, `.captions[key]`) is a SaftUI frame at `FULLSCREEN_DIALOG`, level `EM.BAR.LEVEL`, a `panel` in the theme on the options Background (as the bar), movable, `st:KeepInScreenMargin`ed, taking the mouse (a click on it selects no mover under it); its title frame is the drag handle (`StartMoving`, then at the end the place is read from where it shows, whole, put inside the margin by `st:GetScreenMargin` (the stub does not clamp, so this is code, not the game), saved and anchored). `EM:PlaceBarWindow()` anchors it at the saved place, or by default at `x = floor((UIParent width - 312) / 2)`, `y = -80`, inside the margin; it runs on every layout, on `UI_SCALE_CHANGED`, `DISPLAY_SIZE_CHANGED`, `st.UIScale:OnChanged` (the same function as the events: it lays the bar out, and places the window on the next frame) and `st:UpdateScreenMargin` (hooked), and a saved place the screen no longer has is placed inside without being rewritten. While a mover is dragged it fades to `EM.BAR.DRAG_ALPHA` and every frame of it lets go of the mouse, as the bar does (`bar.lua`), and `Restore` gives both back. The status line is under the Changes section, from the left, wrapped to the inside width and never cut (no tooltip fallback; it is measured with its height set to 0, since a font string drops lines past the height it was given); the window grows by its height and `SECTION_PAD` while it shows (`EM:PlaceWindowStatus`), is placed again as it grows or shrinks, and a hidden window stops any move it was in. A control whose section the window lacks goes in the last one. The History list opens beside the window: its top left 8 right of the window's top right, or its top right 8 left of its top left where that leaves the margin (with room on neither side, the side with more). A mover's position is never changed by docking, so one dragged into the strip the bar covered stays there; with the bar docked again the filter's Enter selection still reaches it.
  - **Exit and Options.** Exit is `EM:Deactivate()`; Blizzard's own Edit Mode is never closed from SaftUI (`EM:IsBlizzardEditModeOpen()` only reads `EditModeManagerFrame:IsShown()`; Exit's tooltip says Blizzard's stays open). Options is `EM:ShowSettings(EM.selected)`, or `st.Config:Open('Fonts', 'editmode')` (Global settings > Edit mode) with none selected, or a selected mover with no `configPath`. Turning edit mode on or off prints nothing; the combat refusals still do.
  - **Undo, Redo, History and Reset** (`Bar/barchanges.lua`, group `changes`). Icon buttons (`undo2`, `redo2`, `history`, `rotateCcw`, tinted like the bar's others), each refreshing on `HistoryChanged`, `Selected` and `PositionChanged`. Undo and Redo are on while `History:CanUndo()` / `CanRedo()`; their tooltips name the mover ("Puts Player back where it was before." / "Moves Player again.") or, for a step with none, its label ("Undoes <label>." / "Redoes <label>."), and a disabled one says "Nothing to undo." in Text > Danger. History is on while the list is open. Controller's ruling from the owner's report of 2026-11-14, pending his confirmation. Holding Ctrl suspends it, and arrow-key nudges ignore it. ## Registering a mover

```lua
st:RegisterMover('key', {
	frame = holderOrFrame,        -- the overlay does SetAllPoints on this; it needs a real size
	label = 'Name',
	getPosition = function() return self.config.position end,   -- read through self.config at call time
	setPosition = function(pos) self.config.position = pos end,
	apply = function() self:ApplyPosition() end,                 -- can run in combat lockdown
	configPath = { 'ModuleName', 'section', 'general' },         -- keys from the /sui root to a leaf group
	blizzardFrame = nil,          -- true only when frame is Blizzard's own, with no holder (the Minimap)
	module = 'Chat',              -- the name the edit mode bar's Show list gives the addon
	isDisabled = function() return not self.config.enable end,   -- turned off by a setting
	isHidden = function() return not frame:IsShown() end,        -- on, but not on screen now
	getDefaultPosition = function() return st.defaults.chat.position end,   -- where Reset goes
	noDefaultReason = nil,        -- a string, why Reset can't run when there is no default
})
```

- **What the edit mode bar reads** (`SaftUI-Core/EditMode/editmode.lua`; all optional to `st:RegisterMover`, but every registration in SaftUI gives them, and `tests/core/editmode_movers_test.lua` scans for it). Core never names a module: every name comes from a registration.
  - **`module`** is the name the Show list shows for the mover's addon, in sentence case: `'Action bars'`, `'Bags'`, `'Chat'`, `'Loot'`, `'Minimap'`, `'Misc'`, `'Tracker'` or `'Unit frames'`. None is `EM.OTHER_MODULE` (`'Other'`); `EM:MoverModule(mover)` reads it.
  - **Shown, disabled, hidden.** `isDisabled` is `function() -> boolean`, true when a setting turns the frame off (SaftUI's or Blizzard's: a disabled bar or unit frame, the totem bar's Hide totem bar, a swing timer Blizzard's setting hides). `isHidden` is true when the frame is on but not on screen now (a closed bag window, no pet, no target, solo party frames, an extra button not active, no loot roll); a faded bar is shown, and Test mode's previews count as shown. It is read only when `isDisabled` is false. A disabled mover shows only with Show disabled on, an enabled hidden one only with Show hidden on. Both only read (`IsShown`, the module's config, a field of Blizzard's), never call Blizzard code that writes (`.agents/taint.md` rule 3). `EM:IsMoverDisabled(mover)` and `EM:IsMoverHidden(mover)` run them with `xpcall` to `geterrorhandler()`: no predicate, an error, a secret value or anything but `true` is false.
  - **`getDefaultPosition`** is `function() -> table | nil`, where Reset puts the frame, and **`noDefaultReason`** a string saying why Reset can't run when there is none (the totem bar's: SaftUI took its place from Blizzard's Edit Mode). `EM:GetDefaultPosition(mover)` is a fresh deep copy on every call, so a write never reaches `st.defaults`. The objective tracker's is Blizzard's default spot (`st:DefaultTrackerPosition()`, which Misc's `DefaultPosition` returns), a fresh table on every call.
  - `st:RegisterConfigMover`'s spec takes **`defaults`**, the twin of `config`: `function() -> table` holding `position` (`function() return st.defaults.chat end`), made into `getDefaultPosition`. A spec with both `defaults` and `getDefaultPosition` errors. A unit frame mover's is `UF.DefaultUnitConfig(unit)` (`SaftUI-Unitframes/unitframes.lua`): `st.defaults.unitframes.profiles['**'][unit]` when it holds a `position`, else the shared `['**']['**']` table.
- **`st:RegisterConfigMover(frame, spec)`** (`SaftUI-Core/EditMode/editmode.lua`) is that call when the position is just the `position` field of a table in the profile, which is nearly every mover. `spec = { key, config, apply, ...}`: `key` is the mover's key; `config` is a function returning the table that holds `position` (`function() return self.config[id] end`), called on every read and write, never once at registration, since a profile change replaces the profile's tables; `apply` as above. `getPosition` returns `config().position` and `setPosition(pos)` stores the table it's given there. Every other field (`label`, `configPath`, `blizzardFrame`, ...) goes to `st:RegisterMover` as it is, and it returns the mover. A mover that does more than that when it reads or writes (the experience bar migrates its position on read, the totem bar reads through `TB:GetPosition`) keeps its own `st:RegisterMover` closures.
- **`st:PlaceAtPosition(frame, position)`** (`SaftUI-Core/api.lua`) is `frame:ClearAllPoints()` then `frame:SetPoint(st:UnpackPoint(position))` with the offsets rounded to whole pixels of `frame` (`st:RoundToPixel`): a saved 5 is 4 pixels at a scale of 0.75 and a saved 1.3333 is 1 pixel at 1, and at a scale of 1 a whole offset is the very number saved. Only the placing rounds: the saved position is never written, so it goes through a scale change and back unchanged. An offset that isn't a number goes as saved (not made a number). The anchor is whatever `position.frame` holds, so a name is the game's to resolve and none anchors to the frame's parent. It doesn't queue: a frame that can't move now (protected, in combat) goes through `st:SafeReposition` around it. A site that rounds its own way (`st:PlaceHolder`, the action bars' `AB:PlaceFrame`), resolves with `st:ResolveFrame` and falls back to `UIParent` (the buffs, the swing timers, the bags), makes the offsets numbers (the bags, edit mode's own `GetAnchor`), or does other work between the clear and the set (`AB:PlaceFrame` sizing from the unpacked point, a `pcall` fallback), keeps its own. The two groups differ in what the frame does in game, which is why there are no flags to pull them into one.
- **The screen edge margin.** `st:RegisterMover` keeps the frame inside it (`st:KeepInScreenMargin`, below), so `frame` is SaftUI's own frame or holder. A mover on a Blizzard frame moved with no holder (the Minimap) sets `blizzardFrame = true`: SaftUI never clamps a Blizzard frame, so only its drags and nudges keep it in.

- **Offsets.** Drags and nudges write pixel-rounded `x_off`/`y_off` into the table from `getPosition()`, call `setPosition`; the position is copied first (`EM:StartDrag`, `EM:Nudge`: the drag's ticks write into the table `getPosition()` returns, so the history's `old` must be a copy made before anything moves); `apply` runs from `EM:CommitPosition` (at drag end and on each nudge), not on the drag's ticks, which move the frame directly and, about every 0.1s, call `setPosition` only to keep an open `/sui` showing the live x/y. Drags keep the frame's anchor point and relative frame. Only the offsets change.
- **`apply`** must go through `st:SafeReposition`, because a secure frame anchored to yours makes it protected in combat.
- **`configPath`** must resolve in `Config:GetOptionsTable`. Module tables are keyed by module name. End the path at a leaf group, usually `general`, since selecting a parent that only holds subgroups shows an empty pane. A pick whose page has no tabs, only inline groups (each action bar's: `{ 'ActionBars', 'bar3' }`), is such a leaf: its inline groups are its sections.
- **Holder pattern.** Use a SaftUI holder frame when the real frame:
  - can be empty or hidden (loot rolls, boss and party/raid groups, closed bag windows),
  - has no size (the micro menu container), or
  - belongs to Blizzard (swing timer, buffs, chat, the extra action button and zone ability).

  Size the holder from the config, place it from the profile, and anchor the real frame to it. The overlay then always has a footprint. `st:PlaceHolder(holder, key, getSize, getPosition)` (`SaftUI-Core/api.lua`) sizes and places a holder through `st:SafeReposition` under `key`, its offsets rounded to whole pixels, reading `getSize()` and `getPosition()` when it runs, so after combat too: chat, the tooltip and the totem bar use it. The buffs and swing timers place theirs without rounding the offsets, so they keep their own.

  A SaftUI frame that's only hidden, and still sized and placed from the profile while hidden, can be its own mover frame: the overlay follows its edges either way. SaftUI's action bars (`SaftUI-ActionBars/options.lua`) move their secure headers this way, also while a bar is disabled or hidden by its Visibility setting, and while the pet bar has no pet or the stance bar no forms (laid out as one button then, so the outline keeps a size). Label the outline when the frame may not be visible (`mover.label:SetText`), as the action bars and the swing timer do. The extra buttons' outlines (`SaftUI-ActionBars/options.lua`) always say when they show, as they mostly don't.
- **The `tracker` mover** (`SaftUI-Tracker/window.lua`, module `'Tracker'`, the eighth name in the module list; `configPath = { 'Tracker', 'general' }`) is a plain `st:RegisterMover`, registered by `TR:BuildWindow` only while SaftUI's tracker is on, so it never shows disabled. Its frame is `TR.holder` (`SaftUITrackerHolder`), Width by Max height, so edit mode's outline covers the whole height the tracker may take; the window hangs from the holder (its top right for Grow down, its bottom right for Grow up) and is never anchored to or by a secure frame, so it is never protected. `apply` is `TR:ApplyHolder()` (`st:PlaceHolder` and `st:KeepInScreenMargin`, then the window again); `isHidden` while the window isn't shown; `getSavedPosition` is `config.position`. **First position:** `tracker.position` has no AceDB default; `TR:GetPosition()` writes it once when the tracker is built, as a copy of the five keys of `objectivetracker.position` when Misc's tracker mover is loaded and the profile has one by a top point (`TOPLEFT`, `TOP`, `TOPRIGHT`), converted to the tracker's `TOPRIGHT` form when it names another point, by arithmetic alone from the profile's data (`ConvertSkinPosition` in `window.lua`: the saved point, relative point and offsets on the screen, the skin holder's `OTP.width` and `OTP.HOLDER_HEIGHT`, UIParent's rect), never from the skin holder's live rect, else `st:DefaultTrackerPosition()` (also for a position relative to a frame other than the screen, or a skin that hasn't set up), and again for a profile with none (a new or reset one). A profile switch or copy runs the modules' `UpdateConfig` in `pairs(st.modules)` order after `EM:ClearPositions` cleared every holder, so a live rect could be stale or empty; the profile's data and the skin's writing nothing while SaftUI's tracker is on make the answer the same in either order. With the switch off it is only read, never written. The `/sui` Position row refuses `SaftUITracker`, `SaftUITrackerHolder` and any frame inside the window.
- **The `trackernearest` mover** (`SaftUI-Tracker/nearest.lua`, module `'Tracker'`, `configPath = { 'Tracker', 'items' }`, label "Nearest quest item") is a plain `st:RegisterMover`, registered by `TR:BuildNearest` (called by `TR:Build`, so only while SaftUI's tracker is on) on the nearest button's holder `SaftUITrackerNearestHolder`, a plain frame made with the tracker even in combat (a `/reload` in combat), LOW strata, level 40, `nearest_size` square (whole pixels). The button, `SaftUITrackerNearestItem`, is a secure child of `SaftUITrackerNearestDriver` (a state driver frame on the holder that hides it in a pet battle), anchored to the holder by one `TOPLEFT` point, and is made out of combat only, the first time the setting is on, so the holder is protected from then on (rule 6 of `.agents/taint.md`): `TR:PlaceNearest` (`st:PlaceHolder`, so `st:SafeReposition` under `'tracker_nearest_holder'`, then `st:KeepInScreenMargin`) places it from `TR.config.items.nearest_position`, now or after combat, and is the mover's `apply`, `TR:UpdateConfig`'s and the Position row's. `isDisabled` is `not TR.config.items.nearest`, `isHidden` is true while the button has no quest item to show (no `questID` on it), and `getDefaultPosition` is a copy of `st.defaults.tracker.items.nearest_position` (`TOPRIGHT` of UIParent, -320 and -275 on Forever or -260 on retail, so left of the tracker's own default spot), a plain AceDB default since there is nothing to inherit. `getPosition` and `setPosition` read and write `items.nearest_position` (the key isn't `position`, so not `st:RegisterConfigMover`). Its Position row refuses (with the reason in the footer's status line) an anchor frame that moves with the tracker's window (`TR.NearestAnchorRefusal`, `st:MovesWith`), because protection spreads from the holder to what it is anchored to and the window is sized in combat (`.agents/taint.md` rule 16); a saved position naming one is placed on UIParent.
- **Taking over a Blizzard Edit Mode frame.** The swing timer bars (`SaftUI-Misc/Skinning/blizzard/Blizzard_SwingTimerPosition.lua`), buffs (`Blizzard_BuffFramePosition.lua`), chat (`SaftUI-Chat/chatframe.lua`), the default tooltip's `GameTooltipDefaultContainer` (`SaftUI-Misc/tooltipposition.lua`; the tooltip itself is never moved) on a shaman, Forever's totem bar `MultiCastActionBarFrame` (`SaftUI-Misc/totemposition.lua`, mover `totembar`), the objective tracker `ObjectiveTrackerFrame` (`SaftUI-Misc/Skinning/blizzard/Blizzard_ObjectiveTrackerPosition.lua`, mover `objectivetracker`) and the armour durability figure `DurabilityFrame` (`SaftUI-Misc/durability.lua`, mover `durability`) all follow the same pattern. The figure's differences: it moves nothing until a position is saved (the holder follows where Blizzard puts it, read on the frame after Blizzard's re-place), it stays in its right container (hung from the container's top right by the same one point Blizzard sets, so the container's layout still counts it), it has no default place for Reset (`noDefaultReason`; Undo's `setPosition(nil)` and the Move the durability figure setting give it back to Blizzard), and its holder is the figure's own 60 by 75:
  - Use a holder.
  - Re-anchor the Blizzard frame with `st:SetPointDirect` / `st:ClearAllPointsDirect` in post-hooks on its `SetPoint` / `ClearAllPoints` / `UpdateSystem`. If it's protected (the totem bar holds secure buttons), the re-anchor waits for the end of combat (`st:QueueAfterCombat`), and the holder goes through `st:SafeReposition`. For a frame anchored over its holder by two corners, `st:HoldFrame(frame, holder, x, y)` (`api.lua`; `x` and `y` outside the holder on each side) gives the anchor and its re-anchor post-hooks on `ClearAllPoints` and `SetPoint`, with the wait for the end of combat on a protected frame; the buffs and the swing timers use it. It hooks no `UpdateSystem` and doesn't place the holder: the site still does that.
  - Where Blizzard's Edit Mode can read the frame's anchor (a snap break, when the frame it's snapped to hides, records it into the layout through `UpdateSystemAnchorInfo`, and a layout save keeps it), don't anchor the frame straight to a SaftUI holder. Place it on UIParent at the holder's spot instead, from the holder's rect, and again whenever the holder moves or resizes (the totem bar's `TB:AnchorBar`, run from a post-hook on the holder's `SetPoint`). Otherwise the layout names the holder, and Blizzard anchors the frame to a missing frame wherever the holder doesn't exist. Chat copies its holder's own anchor instead (`CHT:PlaceChatFrame`), so the layout would name the holder's relative frame, UIParent by default. The objective tracker goes at the holder's spot by its top right corner, anchored to its own parent (UIParent, or Blizzard's `RightManagedFrameContainer` while it's in its default position): Blizzard's `UpdateHeight` there adds the first point's y offset to the parent's height, so the tracker still reaches down to the container's bottom, and a layout that records the anchor names a frame of Blizzard's. Its holder is only the tracker's width and its header's height; SaftUI never sets the tracker's height (its `OnSizeChanged` runs Blizzard's `MarkDirty`, so a size from SaftUI's code would schedule the tracker's layout tainted, and a tainted layout that makes a quest item button writes its `OnClick` tainted for the session; `.agents/taint.md` rule 5), so there is no Height option. `objectivetracker.position` has no default, as the totem bar's: with none saved the holder is at Blizzard's default spot, and a tracker already moved in Blizzard's Edit Mode gives the profile its top right corner on first load (`OTP:CapturePosition`, worked out from the tracker's one anchor and explicit size, never its rect), so first load moves nothing. ## History (`SaftUI-Core/EditMode/history.lua`, `moves.lua`)

The store behind the bar's Undo, Redo, Reset and History, `EM.History`. `history.lua` knows no kind of thing; `moves.lua` is edit mode's side. The bar's controls for it are `Bar/barchanges.lua` and `Bar/historylist.lua` (see The bar). This is part one of the owner's "history tracks every change": what edit mode and bulk actions change. `/sui` settings are part two, not built.

- **A step** is one action: `{ kind = 'move' | 'nudge' | 'reset' | 'bulk' | 'revert', label = string | function(step), time, target = <mover key>, changes = { { kind, where, old, new }, ... }, open, last }`. A drag from press to release is one step (`move`), a Reset is one (`reset`, never merged), a bulk action is one holding everything it changed (`bulk`; the unit frames' Copy from pushes one, and it has no single-step revert). A step or a change with no net change (`same(old, new)`) records nothing.
- **Kinds.** `History:RegisterKind(name, handler)`: `read(where)` a copy of what is stored, `write(where, value)` stores a copy through the setter a player's change uses, optional `finish(wheres)` (once per step after its writes: the position kind runs `def.apply` through `xpcall`, then fires `PositionChanged`), `same`, `place` (the place a change touches, default `name:where`; it decides staleness and revert), `check(where, value) -> ok, why`, `name(where)`, and `missing(where)` (the thing is gone: its part is skipped and reported). The one kind now is `'position'` (`where` is a mover key; `check` refuses an anchor `st:ValidateAnchor` refuses, and prints why). **Part two adds a kind**: register a handler whose `read`/`write` go through the settings' own getter and setter, and push steps with `kind = 'settings'` changes from the place that changes them; nothing in `history.lua` changes.
- **The grouping rule lives in `History.Merges(top, step, now)` and `History.MERGE_GAP` (1 second), nowhere else.** The owner confirmed the rules (`docs/research/2026-11-13-undo-history-systems.md`, the spec's "The history's rules, confirmed"): the top step is an open run of nudges, both are nudges of the same mover, nothing has been undone (`Position() == #steps`), the nudge starts where the run left off (`History.Continues`: a value changed from outside, a `/sui` edit, ends the run) and comes within `MERGE_GAP` of the run's last. A merge matches changes by `place`, not by the identity of `where`. The merged step keeps the first `old` and takes the latest `new`; a run back at its start is removed. A run closes (`History:CloseRun()`) on any other push, selecting another mover (`EM:Select`), `Disabled`, and every undo, redo, jump and revert. A nudge's label is a function of its first change, so a merged run reads its net offset ("Nudged Player by 3, 0").
- **The limit.** `History.limit` is 50, `History:SetLimit(n)` rounds it and clamps to `LIMIT_MIN` 1 to `LIMIT_MAX` 1000 (NaN, text that isn't a number and nil are rejected: the limit stays). Lowering it is lazy: nothing is thrown away until the next step is recorded (a slider dragged down and back, or a typo, loses nothing); `Push` then trims the oldest applied steps first, then, if the redo steps alone are still over, the newest redo steps, so the chain that stays has no gap. Raising it takes effect at once. The saved `history_limit` setting is applied by `EM:ApplyHistoryLimit()`.
- **Undo and redo.** `History:Undo()`, `Redo()` -> `ok, message`; `CanUndo`, `CanRedo`; `JumpTo(n)` (one step at a time, 0 is before every step, stops at the first refusal; each step is checked, written and placed in turn, because a later step's anchor check reads the frames; while it runs `History.batching` is true, and only what listeners are told waits: `HistoryChanged` once, and each kind's optional `flush()` (the position kind fires `PositionChanged` once per mover there). An error in a step is reported once and ends the jump as a refusal with that state cleared; `tests/core/editmode_jump_test.lua` compares a jump with stepping over seeded histories). They refuse in combat ("Can't undo in combat.") and write nothing, so a protected frame is never moved by the history in combat. Out of combat the position kind writes through `setPosition` and `apply` (its `st:SafeReposition`) only, the path a drag's commit takes, and an undo is never recorded as a step. A new push after an undo discards the redo steps; there is no branching.
- **Staleness.** Before writing, every change's current value must equal the step's `new` (`old` for a redo), compared with `same`. If not, the step and every older step touching one of its places are dropped, nothing is written, and the message says so. A refused `check` drops that step only. A thing that is `missing` is skipped and named in the message: a bulk step with a missing part restores every other part whole and says so. A `write` that errors (reported to the error handler) rolls the whole step back (what was written is written back and placed again, `Undo` returns `false`, the step stays applied), so the history and the layout agree. Values are written exactly: `false` and `nil` are values (the store never uses `a and b or c` on one).
- **Revert.** `History:CanRevert(i) -> ok, why` and `History:Revert(i)`: puts one older applied step's `old` values back as a new `revert` step, only while no later applied step touches its place and it isn't stale. A step that isn't applied shows no control (`false, nil`).
- **Clears** on an AceDB profile change, copy or reset (`OnProfileChanged`, `OnProfileCopied`, `OnProfileReset`, registered on the first push), never on a theme change or leaving edit mode. History is per profile: a step of one profile is never applied to another. A drag dropped by a profile change records nothing.
- **The objective tracker's mover stands down while SaftUI's own tracker owns Blizzard's** (`SaftUI-Tracker/blizzard.lua`; `OTP`'s local `Owned()` asks `st:GetModule('Tracker', true):OwnsBlizzard()` at call time, which latches on its first call, so it may ask before the Tracker module enables). `SaftUI-Tracker` conceals Blizzard's tracker off screen by its one anchor, so `OTP:PlaceTracker`, `KeepInMargin`, `UpdateFootprint`, `CapturePosition`, `KeepPlacement` and `TrackerReach` return at once, the mover's `isDisabled` is `Owned` (it shows only under Show disabled in edit mode), the page's Position is `disabled` with "SaftUI's tracker is on (Tracker page): Blizzard's tracker shows in its place when it's needed.", the holder is still made and placed and its saved position is never touched (no capture, no `KeepPlacement`: a profile with none stays none for it), and its hooks stay and do nothing. Its page and its mover are named "Blizzard's tracker" (the page's desc: "Blizzard's objective tracker: its fonts, and its place while SaftUI's tracker is off. Its fonts also style it while it fills in for SaftUI's (Tracker page)."), so Reset's reason reads "Blizzard's tracker is already in its default place."; Skinning's toggle keeps its name, Objective tracker. With the switch off everything here is as it always was. The corner math is Core's `st:CornerOffset(frame, parent, point, screenX, screenY)` (`api.lua`), used by `OTP:PlaceTracker`, `TR:BlizzardTarget` and the tracker's first position.
- **Saved position.** A mover that makes up a position while none is saved (the totem bar, the objective tracker) gives `def.getSavedPosition` (nil then). The position kind reads it (`EM.SavedPosition`), so undoing the first move writes `setPosition(nil)` and puts "nothing saved" back; such a `setPosition` must accept nil and forget where the holder was placed (`KeepPlacement` would save that spot again).
- **Positions.** `EM.CopyPosition` (deep, a plain table that also holds the fields the profile only has through AceDB's defaults), `EM.SamePosition` (point, `rel_point` defaulting to the point, the frame with nil, `'UIParent'` and UIParent one, offsets as numbers within 1e-4), `EM.MoveLabel`, `EM:RecordMove(mover, kind, before)`, `EM:CanReset(mover) -> ok, why`, `EM:Reset(mover) -> ok, message` (sets a copy of `EM:GetDefaultPosition(mover)` through `setPosition`, runs `apply` and fires `PositionChanged`, then records). `HistoryChanged` is the event (no arguments) on every push, merge, undo, redo, jump, revert, trim and clear.
- **Copy from is one step** (`SaftUI-Unitframes/config/config.lua`). `UF:CopySettings(unit, from, keys) -> step | nil` copies each key's deep copy from `from` onto `unit` (in the profile in use), the old values copied first, then calls `UF:UpdateConfig()`, then pushes ONE `bulk` step 'Copied <from> to <unit>' with a change per key whose value changed (none changed: no step). The dialog's Confirm copy calls it with the ticked keys (sorted), then `st.Config:Refresh()` and `CloseDialog`. It offers and copies only the settings both units' pages name (`CopyNames` for both; a setting is hidden once the chosen source's page lacks it, and a key one page lacks is skipped), since the source's config has no value for one its page lacks. The `'ufsetting'` kind (registered at load when edit mode is there, and again before each push): `where = { profile, unit, key }`; `read` is a deep copy of what is stored, `write` stores a deep copy (false and nil exact, safe to run again on the same place: the history rolls a failed step back with it); `finish` runs `UF:UpdateConfig()` then `st.Config:Refresh()`; `place` is `'ufsetting:<profile>:<unit>:<key>'`, `name` the unit's label; `check` refuses a profile that isn't the one in use ('The unit frame profile changed since.'); `missing` is a deleted profile (a deleted unit can't be told: indexing it through the profile's `['**']` makes it again). The profile is found with `rawget` (reading a missing one from `profiles` would make it).
- **The unit frame profile switch clears the history** (`profiles.lua`: `SwitchProfile` (New, Copy, Rename, Import), `UF:SetProfile`, Delete and the select's `set`, the footer's and the dialog's), when the profile in use actually changes: a step of one unit frame profile (a Copy from, a unit frame's mover position) must never be applied to another. A pick of the profile already in use clears nothing.
- **Tests.** `tests/core/editmode_history_test.lua` (the store, on a test kind) and `tests/core/editmode_moves_test.lua` (movers); `tests/core/editmode_bar_changes_test.lua` (the changes group, the status line, the list) and `tests/unitframes/copy_from_history_test.lua` (Copy from as a step, the profile clears); the objective tracker and totem bar tests cover their Reset.
- **History rows (interaction states).** A row is a state frame of shape `'row'` (`inset = 0`, `text = row.label`, `textRest` the label's own Muted or Normal, `blizzard` the list's): it tints under the mouse and the current step is the `selected` row (the bar and the fade; the 2px mark is gone). `FillRow` sets `selected` every time it fills a row, and the list's `OnHide` clears it, so a hidden list costs a pass of the Accent nothing. Under Blizzard `row.hover` is the dropdown list's `rowHover` art as before, and, since Blizzard's art draws no mark, the current step's label is the accent (`LabelColor`). `RecolorList(group)` puts a label's color back and, for a Text pass, `st:PaintStates(row)` so a label under a tint picks its color again. `EM.RecolorNumberBox(box)` colors only the arrows' Muted: the edit box's hover and focus edge are the kit's (`W.NumberBox`). ## Screen edge margin

`misc.clamp_inset` (default `st.CLAMP_INSET`, 20), Screen edge margin on Global settings' General tab, is how far in from the screen's edges SaftUI keeps the frames the user can move (below 0, how far past them they can go). It's measured by the frame's rect, not its outline or border, in the frame's own units (UIParent's for SaftUI's frames), and it is a whole number of the frame's pixels: 20 is 31 pixels on a Mac screen (a scale of 1.5514), and 20 where a unit is a pixel (`st:GetScreenMargin` rounds it with `st:RoundToPixel`; the reach a frame gives is added as it is). Every scale change runs `st:UpdateScreenMargin()` (the pass), so the clamp follows the new pixel. The reaches that cancel the margin (`HeldFrameReach` in `api.lua`, `DUR:MarginReach`, the objective tracker's `TrackerReach`) subtract the same rounded number, so margin plus reach is exact. What still reads the plain `misc.clamp_inset`: `/sui`'s `OW.MaxWidth` and `OW.MaxHeight` (`optionslayout.lua`) and SaftUI's tracker's `TR:MaxHeight` (`SaftUI-Tracker/window.lua`), both size limits, until phase 2. The defaults sit at least that far in by their rects (the buffs at `-st.CLAMP_INSET`), so first load pushes nothing.
- **How.** `st:KeepInScreenMargin(frame, reach)` (`SaftUI-Core/api.lua`) clamps the frame to the screen with insets of `-m, m, m, -m` (the game keeps its rect, grown by them, on screen) and remembers it. `reach` is optional: a function returning how far the frame's art reaches past its left, right, top and bottom edges, read each time the margin is applied, which grows the insets so the art stays inside the margin and not only the rect. A holder with a Blizzard frame on it uses it for how much further in the frame's own clamp keeps it (`st:KeepHolderInScreenMargin`, above). `st:GetScreenMargin(frame)` is the margin plus the reach on each side, which the game's clamp and edit mode's drags both keep. Calling it again for a frame re-applies the margin with the current reach (`/sui`'s window does, from `Relayout`). `st:UpdateScreenMargin()` re-applies the margin to every frame it remembers: the General tab's row and a profile switch (`Config:UpdateConfig`, before the modules place their frames) run it. Both are blocked in combat on a protected frame, which keeps its margin until combat ends; one queued re-apply (`QueueAfterCombat('ScreenMargin')`) then does them all.
- **Who gets it.** Every mover's frame (`st:RegisterMover`), holders included; every window with a header (`st:CreateHeader`: the bags and bank); `/sui`'s window and dialog; and the holders of the Blizzard frames SaftUI moves, which follow them (see the takeover pattern above). It holds a window's rect, not the metal's title bar above it under the Blizzard theme, where its header (title, buttons and drag handle) sits: at a margin under 20 that can go off the top of the screen (`docs/followups.md`, "Blizzard theme: in-game checks"). `/sui`'s window is the exception: it passes `reach` (`ThemeArt.MetalReach()` under Blizzard), so its metal stays inside the margin and the title bar can't leave the screen.
- **Who doesn't.** Blizzard's frames. The Minimap (its mover sets `blizzardFrame`) keeps to the margin only when dragged or nudged. `st:EnableMoving` only keeps its frames on screen: every caller hands it a Blizzard UI panel (`SettingsPanel`, `AddonList`, `TransmogFrame`, `WorldMapFrame`, `TimeManagerFrame`). ChatFrame1's insets are Blizzard's Edit Mode's; chat follows its holder instead, which goes no nearer the edges than those insets let ChatFrame1 go.
- **Where it shows.** The game shows a frame anchored into the margin at the margin, and leaves its anchor as set: a Position typed in `/sui`, or saved before the margin, stays in the profile. `st:GetClampShift(frame)` is how far the clamp moved it, so a drag or nudge starts from where it shows and stores that; chat adds it to ChatFrame1's copied anchor.

Tests: `tests/core/screen_margin_test.lua` (who gets it, when it's re-applied, combat, Blizzard frames, drags and nudges, `st:GetClampShift`, holders with a Blizzard frame on them), `tests/chat/screen_margin_test.lua` (chat's outline and ChatFrame1 stopping together), `tests/misc/tooltip_margin_test.lua` (the tooltip's holder), `tests/misc/swing_timer_margin_test.lua` (the swing timers' holders), `tests/core/general_tab_test.lua` (the row) and `tests/misc/screen_margin_defaults_test.lua` (the buffs' default).

## `/sui` options

- `/sui` is SaftUI's own window (`st.OptionsWindow`, below), drawn from the tree `Config:GetOptionsTable` builds from each module's `GetConfigTable()`. AceConfigDialog no longer draws it.
- `st.Config:Open(...path)` rebuilds the tree, registers it with AceConfigRegistry as `st.name`, and opens the window at the path's page, picker value and tab (`OM.SelectionForPath`). `st.Config:Close()` closes it, and `st.Config:GetWindow()` is its frame while it's open, else nil. `st.Config:Refresh()` (AceConfigRegistry's NotifyChange) re-renders an open window on the next frame.
- **How a table maps onto the window.** The model (`SaftUI-Core/Config/optionsmodel.lua`, `st.OptionsModel`) reads AceConfig option tables as AceConfig defines them, so a module's `GetConfigTable` is a plain AceConfig table:
  - A top-level group is a sidebar page: its `name` is the page title and its `desc` the line under it.
  - The page's child groups that aren't inline are its tabs (whatever its `childGroups`, except `'select'`, which makes them the picker). Inside the chosen tab or pick, child groups that aren't inline are a second tab row, or the picker when that group says `'select'`. Anything deeper is drawn as sections on the page; nothing is dropped.
  - Inline groups (`inline = true`, or the parent's `childGroups = 'inline'`) are sections titled with their `name`, with any `desc` as a note. A `header` starts a section titled with its name. Options after a nested group continue in an untitled section.
  - Options are rows in `order` (100 by default, negative orders last, then by name), packed by `size`: `'small'` (toggle, execute, input and item only; a color is never small, its field being a dropdown's width) takes a quarter of a line, two to a half; no `size` is normal and takes a half; `'wide'`, and every `description` and `position` row, take a line of their own. Ace's `width` is not read. An execute's `align` (SaftUI's field, `'right'` or `'center'`) makes it a button lined up with the aligned buttons next to it, at its own width (see the body's line of buttons, below).
  - Hidden and `guiHidden` options are left out; disabled ones are dimmed and don't respond. A disabled control (but a multiselect, whose rows keep the mouse) takes no mouse, so hovering it shows its row's tooltip, where an option's own `disabledDesc` (SaftUI's: a string, or a function called with `info`; never inherited, read only while it's disabled) says why, under the desc in Text > Danger. `get`, `set`, `func`, `validate`, `confirm`, `disabled`, `hidden` and `handler` are inherited from parent groups, method-name strings resolve on the handler, and function-valued `name`, `desc`, `values`, `disabled` and `hidden` are called with an AceConfigDialog-style `info`.
  - A failed `validate` shows in the footer's status line (an input keeps the text typed). `confirm` asks in SaftUI's confirm dialog. A `set` re-renders the page.
  - Each type has a widget: toggle, range (slider and value box), select (a dropdown; `dialogControl = 'LSM30_Font'` lists each font in its own face), multiselect (checkboxes), input, color (a field as wide and tall as a dropdown, drawn as an input is: a swatch at its left, 40 wide and the field's inner height (a black line round the color, a see-through color, under 100%, drawn over a checker of two greys in 4px squares, a solid one over none), the field's edge as a 1px line after it, then the hex code 8 after that, left-aligned, in the field font and the normal text color, the same on every color: `#RRGGBB`, or `#RRGGBB · 60%` with `hasAlpha` under 100%; hover lights its edge as an input's; a click anywhere on it opens SaftUI's own color picker, below), execute (a button), keybinding, item (SaftUI's own type: a Show box, its name and Lucide's ellipsis-vertical dots that open a menu of its settings; see Menu items, below), description (its text, and AceConfigDialog's image: `image`, `imageCoords`, `imageWidth` and `imageHeight`, the image at the row's left and the text to its right; SaftUI's `justifyH`, `'LEFT'` (the default), `'CENTER'` or `'RIGHT'`, sets the text across the row, and SaftUI's `fill` makes its row a fill row, below, with what it shows in the middle).
  - **A footer control** (in the kit, and unused: no page draws one since the unit frames' profile moved to the Unit dropdown's line). A page's top-level group may set `footer` (SaftUI's field, never in its `args`): a group (`type = 'group'`, a `name`, `args`) whose options the window draws in its footer's region (left of Reload UI), while that page is open (`OM.BuildView` hands it on as `view.footer`). Its name is the one label (field font, Text > Muted), then each option's widget in order, 8 apart, with the options kit's own widgets (`W.Create` and `Bind`, from a tree of its own, as the picker's), so it takes both looks; it's meant for a select and buttons, and the select's own name isn't drawn. A set draws the page again, as a row's does; `hidden`, `disabled` and the inherited members work as on a page. ## Ace3's own windows

SaftUI doesn't use AceConfigDialog or AceGUI: `/sui`, its dialog and every option widget are SaftUI's. Core's TOCs still load both (with the LibSharedMedia AceGUI widgets) as libraries for other addons. SaftUI's reskin of them (`aceskin.lua`) is gone; it only ever styled SaftUI's own windows, so other addons' Ace windows look as they did. Don't hook `AceGUI:Create`, a widget constructor or `AceGUI:Release`: AceGUI's widgets are pooled and shared with every addon.
