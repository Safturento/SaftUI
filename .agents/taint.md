# Taint and combat safety

WoW marks every Lua value written by addon code as *tainted* by that addon. Secure Blizzard code that later reads a tainted value runs tainted, and if it then makes a protected call (moving or showing an action bar, `SetAttribute` on a secure button, etc.), the game blocks it with `ADDON_ACTION_BLOCKED` blamed on SaftUI. The error usually fires far from the cause, often in combat, so these rules are strict.

Third-party addon frames (RestedXP Guides in `SaftUI-Misc/Skinning/restedxp.lua`) aren't Blizzard frames, so rule 1 doesn't bind them. SaftUI still only adds to their scripts (hooks, never `SetScript`) and keeps its state in side tables keyed by their frames.
The RestedXP skin stands down for RestedXP's v2 window because RestedXP hides the v1 frames itself, and it relies on RestedXP re-anchoring `CurrentStepFrame` (hence a `SetPoint` post-hook that keeps SaftUI's gap) and re-`SetScript`ing the list steps on every guide load (hence hover follows their `SetBackdropColor` calls). RestedXP sizes `GuideName` and `Footer` only once, at load, so the skin grows them to fit a long or two-line title and its timer bars. The title's `GetStringHeight` counts its wrapping only once WoW has laid the text out, which the welcome text (set during load) and a window resize both come before, so the skin refits from the `OnSizeChanged` of a frame pinned to the text, not from the box's own. Those timers are LibCandyBar bars, which LibCandyBar recycles between every addon that uses it (BigWigs, for one). The skin styles only bars in RestedXP's `BarContainer.bars`, and on `LibCandyBar_Stop` it hides its boxes. LibCandyBar resets everything else when it hands a bar out again. Without LibCandyBar's callback it leaves the timers alone.

## Rules

1. **Never write Lua fields or methods onto a Blizzard frame or table.** That includes:
   - `frame.backdrop` via `st:SetBackdrop(blizzardFrame)`
   - `frame.SetFoo = st.dummy`
   - `st:Kill` (it replaces `Show`)
   - your own `frame.width`
   - adding keys to Blizzard tables

   Keep SaftUI's per-frame data in a local weak table (`setmetatable({}, { __mode = 'k' })`) or on a SaftUI-owned frame. Writing fields onto frames SaftUI created (including oUF frames it spawned) is fine.

   The skinning helpers write fields too: `st:SetBackdrop` (`frame.backdrop`, `frame.thick`), `st:SkinActionButton` (that, plus `button.hover` and the like) and `st:SkinIcon` (a hook on the icon: since `28c0448` it keeps its crop and its hooked flag in weak tables of `skinning.lua`'s, so it writes nothing onto the icon but its hook). Use them on SaftUI's own buttons only. Under the Blizzard theme `st:SetBackdrop` also makes a button's ring as a texture of the frame it draws behind, so a caller that still draws on someone else's frame with `st:SetBackdrop` or `st:SkinActionButton` marks it first with `st:MarkForeign(frame)` (`SaftUI-Core/theme.lua`), which writes nothing onto it and keeps the ring on a frame of SaftUI's: the bags bar's Blizzard bag slots (`SaftUI-Bags/containers/bag.lua`) and Hekili's buttons (`SaftUI-Misc/Skinning/hekili.lua`), which also still take those fields. To skin one of Blizzard's buttons, as `SaftUI-Misc/totemskin.lua` does for the totem bar's secure buttons and `SaftUI-Misc/Skinning/Blizzard_CooldownViewer.lua` for the Cooldown Manager's items:
   - Keep SaftUI's pieces in the weak table, keyed by Blizzard's button.
   - The backdrop is a frame of SaftUI's (`st:CreateFrame` + `st:SetTemplate`), made as the button's child one frame level below it.
   - Hover, pushed and checked textures are made on the button and set with `SetHighlightTexture`/`SetPushedTexture`/`SetCheckedTexture`, out of combat. They are flat under Thick and Thin and Blizzard's state art under Blizzard (`st.ThemeArt.StyleState`), which only changes SaftUI's own textures (and the button's `Flash` region), so it holds in combat. The hover stays in the HIGHLIGHT layer in every look (`st.ThemeArt.HOVER_LAYER`): the client shows that layer only under the mouse, so a hover moved out of it shows all the time.
   - Draw the backdrop with `st:SetTemplate(backdrop, theme, background, kind, button)`, naming the button as host: under Blizzard the ring and own-color piece are then textures of the button, between its icon and its texts, not a holder frame over them.
   - The element buttons are two stacked buttons, and the action button on top (`MultiCastActionButton`, which inherits `ActionButtonTemplate`'s `TextOverlayContainer` at level 500) covers the slot button's ring. So under Blizzard each action button also gets a ring frame of SaftUI's (`st:MarkForeign` + `st:SetBackdropIn`, kind `action`, drawn only under Blizzard) whose holder sits one level over it, over its icon and under its texts. `MultiCastSlotButton_Update`'s hook colors it with the slot's element colour (`st:SetBorderColor`), for all three pages.
   - Fade Blizzard's other art with `SetAlpha(0)`, but only regions whose alpha Blizzard never sets. `ActionBarActionButtonMixin:UpdateFlash` sets the checked texture's alpha and `SpellHighlightAnim` animates `SpellHighlightTexture`'s, so swap the state textures instead and keep the spell highlight, over the icon.
   - Crop the icon with `SetTexCoord`. Re-crop it in a post-hook wherever Blizzard resets its tex coords.
   - The Cooldown Manager's items show where the recipe beats the alternatives. Each item's backdrop is a frame of SaftUI's 2px inside it (where Blizzard's round icon sat; Blizzard lays the items out Icon Padding less 4 apart so those icons touch), drawn with the item (a buff bar's: its icon frame) as host, and the icon moves to BACKGROUND (`SetDrawLayer`, which Blizzard never calls on it), so under Blizzard the ring, a texture of the item in ARTWORK, lies over the icon and under every frame Blizzard puts on the item: the cooldown, the count, the debuff border and the proc and pandemic glows, which Blizzard makes a level over the item, and a bar's stack count, a font string of the icon frame in OVERLAY. An earlier build drew through `st:SetBackdropIn`, whose ring sits on ThemeArt's holder one level over the item, the same level as those frames; raising the cooldown and count over it left the glows under the swipe. Its out of range shadow (Blizzard's round art) is faded by alpha; Blizzard only shows or hides it, and tints the icon red besides.

   To draw SaftUI's theme behind a frame that isn't SaftUI's, use `st:SetBackdropIn(backdrops, frame, theme, background, kind)` (`SaftUI-Core/theme.lua`). The backdrop is a frame of SaftUI's kept in `backdrops`, the caller's weak table keyed by `frame`, and nothing is written onto `frame`. The kind is an argument, never a field on someone else's frame. Made there, the backdrop is `frame`'s child one level under it. A caller that wants it somewhere else makes its own SaftUI frame and puts it in `backdrops[frame]` first; `st:SetBackdropIn` then draws on that one and places it the theme's border outside `frame`, and a theme redraw moves it with the theme. That's also how to draw behind a texture, which has no frame level to make one under:
   - the buff skin (`SaftUI-Misc/Skinning/Blizzard_BuffFrame.lua`) keys its backdrops by Blizzard's icon texture, each a child of the button one level under it;
   - the micro menu (`SaftUI-Minimap/microbuttons.lua`) makes each Blizzard micro button's panel first, set to ignore the button's alpha, in its `skins` table.

   The tooltip skin (`SaftUI-Misc/tooltip.lua`) and the swing timers (`Blizzard_SwingTimer.lua`) draw through it with tables of their own. So does the Cooldown Manager skin for its tracked bars (`SaftUI-Misc/Skinning/Blizzard_CooldownViewer.lua`); its icons follow the recipe for Blizzard's buttons above. Other per-frame state goes in side tables the same way: the zone text's flourishes are `ZT.flourishes` (`SaftUI-Misc/Skinning/ZoneText.lua`), not fields on `ZoneTextFrame`.
2. **Hook only with post-hooks:** `hooksecurefunc(...)`, native `frame:HookScript(...)`, or AceHook `SecureHook`/`SecureHookScript`. Never AceHook `Hook`/`RawHook`/`HookScript`, which install closures with `SetScript` and replace Blizzard's handlers. Never `SetScript` on a Blizzard frame.
   - AceHook's `HookScript` is also a pre-hook: SaftUI's handler runs before Blizzard's. The zone text's did, until it moved to `ZoneTextFrame:HookScript`; its difficulty line had relied on seeing the zone before Blizzard's handler updated it (`SaftUI-Misc/Skinning/ZoneText.lua`). Moving to a post-hook can change what a handler sees.
   - A frame built from a mixin (XML `mixin=`, `Mixin`, `CreateFromMixins`) has the mixin's methods copied onto it, so hooking the mixin table once the frame exists reaches nothing. Hook what's actually called: the frame's own method or script. The world map's zone label is one: SaftUI finds the label through `WorldMapFrame.dataProviders` (the provider whose `OnSetAreaLabel` is `AreaLabelDataProviderMixin`'s) and hooks its `OnUpdate` with `HookScript`, adding a font string of its own (`ZT:HookWorldMap`). The Cooldown Manager's viewers are another: their `RefreshLayout` hands each item it takes from the viewer's frame pool to `self:OnAcquireItemFrame(item)`, so the skin post-hooks each viewer's own `OnAcquireItemFrame` (the buff bar viewer's overrides the base one; the frame's field is whichever it has), and skins the items a viewer already shows from its pool (`itemFramePool:EnumerateActive()`, a read through the secure pool's proxy).
   - A post-hook runs in the middle of Blizzard's own code: an error in it stops the caller there. The Cooldown Manager's runs inside `RefreshLayout`, before the items get their data and are laid out, so the skin runs each item in `xpcall` to `geterrorhandler()`: the error is reported, Blizzard's layout completes, and the item, marked skinned only once it's styled, is tried again the next time it's handed out.
3. **Don't run Blizzard callbacks from SaftUI code.** Calling a Blizzard function that writes Blizzard state (e.g. `HelpTip:HideAllSystem`, a frame's `Update()`, alert or close callbacks) runs that code in SaftUI's tainted context and taints what it writes. Prefer re-applying SaftUI's own state in a post-hook after Blizzard runs.
   - Calling one that only reads is fine (the world map's `GetMapID` and `GetNormalizedCursorPosition`, which the zone label hook calls).
   - An open case: the zone text sets its fade times with Blizzard's `FadingFrame_SetFadeInTime` and the other two setters, which write fields onto `ZoneTextFrame` and `SubZoneTextFrame`. Only their own `FadingFrame_OnUpdate` reads them (`docs/followups.md`, "Zone text's fade times are written onto Blizzard's frames").
4. **Moving or showing a frame can run its scripts.** `SetParent`, `Show` and `Hide` on a visible frame fire `OnShow`/`OnHide` in your call chain. For Blizzard secure buttons that writes tainted state. Only reparent or hide when it won't change on-screen visibility, otherwise apply at the next `/reload` (see `SaftUI-Misc/totembar.lua`).
5. **Blizzard Edit Mode system frames have Lua overrides** for `SetPoint`, `ClearAllPoints`, `SetScale`, `SetShown` and `Hide` that write Edit Mode state (e.g. `EditModeManagerFrame.editModeSystemAnchorDirty`). Examples: ChatFrame1, BuffFrame/DebuffFrame, QueueStatusButton (Forever), swing timer bars, GameTooltipDefaultContainer (where the default tooltip anchors), the totem bar, MinimapCluster, the micro menu, the status tracking bars.
   - Move them with `st:SetPointDirect` / `st:ClearAllPointsDirect` / `st:SetSizeDirect`. These call the C widget methods via `getmetatable(frame).__index`.
   - Never call the overridden methods from SaftUI code.
   - They also set their own clamp insets, from their `Selection` box, on every layout apply and Edit Mode enter (`EditModeSystemMixin:UpdateClampOffsets`). So SaftUI's screen edge margin (`st:KeepInScreenMargin`, `.agents/edit-mode.md`) goes on SaftUI's own frames and holders only, never on a Blizzard frame. A Blizzard frame anchored to a holder follows the holder's clamp. One that only copies the holder's anchor (ChatFrame1) adds `st:GetClampShift(holder)`, as `CHT:PlaceChatFrame` does.
   - To keep a position, post-hook their `SetPoint`/`ClearAllPoints` (or `UpdateSystem`) and re-apply with the direct helpers. Hide their Edit Mode box with `frame.Selection:HookScript('OnShow', function(s) s:Hide() end)`. On a protected one (the totem bar, which holds secure buttons) the re-apply in combat goes through `st:QueueAfterCombat`, so the frame sits at Blizzard's spot until the fight ends. For the totem bar that's only its empty, click-through frame: its buttons are anchored to SaftUI's holder and stay put, apart from the two exceptions in `.agents/edit-mode.md` (`SaftUI-Misc/totembar.lua`).
6. **Combat:**
   - Secure frames (unit frames, action buttons, group headers) can't be moved, sized, shown, hidden, reparented or have attributes set in combat. Neither can any frame a secure frame is anchored to, which becomes implicitly protected.
   - Route every move through `st:SafeReposition(frame, key, fn)`, which runs `fn` now or after combat. Check with `st:CanMoveNow(frame)`. Defer other work with `st:QueueAfterCombat(key, fn)`.
   - Key binding calls are blocked in combat too: `SetBinding`, `SaveBindings`, `SetOverrideBindingClick` and the other `SetOverrideBinding*`, and `ClearOverrideBindings`. Queue them with `st:QueueAfterCombat`, as `SaftUI-ActionBars/bindings.lua` does.
   - `RegisterStateDriver` sets attributes, so a state driver can't be changed in combat either. A driver that's set keeps showing, hiding and paging its frame in combat with no addon Lua (`[combat] show; hide` shows a bar only in combat).
   - A driver can also hand a token to a snippet (`_onstate-<name>`), which can call the restricted environment's functions and set attributes on frame refs in combat. SaftUI's leave vehicle button shows by `CanExitVehicle()` that way, the check Blizzard's own leave button uses, which covers flight paths. Whether `[canexitvehicle]` alone covers them isn't confirmed. The same snippet shows it on `[possessbar]` and switches its secure type for a possess (`SaftUI-ActionBars/extras.lua`).
   - Alpha isn't protected. `SetAlpha` on a secure frame works in combat, so a fade that only changes alpha is combat-safe (the action bars' mouseover fade in `SaftUI-ActionBars/bars.lua`).
   - Protection spreads from a protected frame to its parents and to the frames it's anchored to, not to its children or to frames anchored to it. So a SaftUI frame made as a child of a protected frame (secure, or protected through an anchor), or anchored to one, can still be shown, hidden, sized and anchored in combat. The totem timers are children of the totem bar's slot buttons, which are plain CheckButtons protected only through an anchor: the secure recall button is anchored to the last shown slot button, and the slot buttons chain from there. Blizzard frames with no secure template, no secure children and nothing secure anchored to them aren't protected either: the totem bar's flyout and its open arrow are made taller, and the flyout's choices sized, from post-hooks in combat (`SaftUI-Misc/totemtimers.lua`, `totemskin.lua`). Not yet confirmed in game.
   - Protected actions (casting, `CastPetAction`, `CastShapeshiftForm`, pet commands) can't be called from addon code at all, in or out of combat. Do them from a `SecureActionButtonTemplate` button: a type that calls the function (`pet`, `spell`), or a secure macro command (`macro` with `macrotext`, e.g. `/petautocasttoggle <name>`) when there's no type for it. Set the attributes out of combat. A secure pre-click snippet (`header:WrapScript(button, 'OnClick', snippet)`) can stop a click in combat by returning false. See `SaftUI-ActionBars/petbar.lua` and `stancebar.lua`.
   - When only a Blizzard button's own OnClick makes the call, the `click` type (`clickbutton` set to that button) clicks it from secure code, even while it's hidden, so Blizzard's Lua runs untainted (not yet confirmed in game for a button inside a roleset-blocked bar). SaftUI's leave vehicle button cancels a possess by clicking Blizzard's `PossessButton2`, whose OnClick calls `CancelPetPossess` (`SaftUI-ActionBars/extras.lua`). On a secure button, `Enable` and `Disable` are protected in combat too; the restricted handles have them.
   - Blizzard's own code moves some protected frames in combat. `ExtraActionBarFrame` holds a secure button, and Blizzard's container lays it out again each time a boss hands out the extra action button, usually mid-fight. A post-hook can't move it back until combat ends. Where Blizzard never re-anchors something inside the frame, anchor that to SaftUI's holder once, out of combat: it then stays put whatever Blizzard does to the frame (`SaftUI-ActionBars/extras.lua`, and `.agents/edit-mode.md`). The totem bar's buttons are anchored to its holder the same way, so when Blizzard re-places the bar in combat only its empty frame moves, made click-through so it can't catch clicks there. Blizzard's own button update re-anchors the first element and the summon pages to the bar, so a fight that runs it while the bar is elsewhere shows those, and the buttons chained from them, at the bar until combat ends (`SaftUI-Misc/totembar.lua`).
   - To keep SaftUI's own layout on protected frames that Blizzard re-anchors, possibly in combat: post-hook the Blizzard function that re-anchors them and re-apply the layout, straight away out of combat and through `st:QueueAfterCombat` in combat. Blizzard's layout then shows until the fight ends. Anchor by the same points Blizzard re-sets, so its `SetPoint` replaces SaftUI's anchor rather than adding a second one that pulls against it. The totem bar's button size and spacing work this way, after `MultiCastSummonSpellButton_Update` and `MultiCastRecallSpellButton_Update` (`SaftUI-Misc/totembar.lua`).
   - A SaftUI frame that is a child of a secure header and anchored to it follows the header's in-combat layout by its anchors alone, with no SaftUI code: the unit frames' group border (`SaftUI-Unitframes/groups.lua`, `UF:GroupBorder`) is a plain unprotected frame, the party, raid10 or raid40 header's child at its level, anchored a few pixels outside it. It is shown, hidden and drawn in combat without a protected call and nothing is written onto the header. A header can be shown with no member to show (the party's, at a pending invite with Show player off, or while a group disbands) and then sizes itself to a sliver, so the border hides its art by alpha from its own `OnSizeChanged` (SaftUI code on SaftUI's own frame, alpha isn't protected, a secret size is left alone).
   - Edit mode, `/sui` and the action bars' keybind mode close on `PLAYER_REGEN_DISABLED` and refuse to open in combat.
7. **No arithmetic or comparisons on secret values.** See `.agents/clients.md`.
8. **To hide a Blizzard frame for good**, prefer `frame:SetRolesets('alwaysBlocked')`, as oUF does for party frames and `SaftUI-Unitframes/groups.lua` does for `CompactRaidFrameContainer`. It's one C call that writes nothing onto the frame, and the frame stops drawing whatever Blizzard's own Show/Hide calls do. It's still bound by rule 4: apply it only while the frame isn't on screen, and only out of combat.
   - A frame that's on screen when SaftUI loads (Blizzard's `MainActionBar` always, `PetActionBar` after a `/reload` with a pet out) can be concealed instead, without changing its visibility: `SetAlpha(0)` and `EnableMouse(false)` on it and its mouse-enabled children, called through `getmetatable(frame).__index`. See `SaftUI-ActionBars/hideblizzard.lua`.
   - A font string or texture of Blizzard's whose alpha Blizzard never sets can be concealed the same way, with `SetAlpha(0)`, and stays where it is. The zone text's PvP line (`PVPInfoTextString`, "(Contested Territory)") is, so SaftUI's level and faction line can take its place; Blizzard keeps writing it, and after each run of Blizzard's zone text handler (which anchors the subzone name under the PvP line) SaftUI anchors the subzone name under its own line. A replacement has to follow whatever clears the original too: banners empty Blizzard's zone text through `ZoneText_Clear`, so SaftUI post-hooks it (`hooksecurefunc`) to empty its own line and difficulty line and hide its flourishes until the next zone name.
   - Blocking only stops the drawing. Blizzard's Lua for the frame keeps running on its events, and it can move other frames into it, which then vanish too. In a skinned vehicle, `OverrideActionBar`'s `UNIT_ENTERED_VEHICLE` handler reparents Blizzard's micro menu into the bar, which would take SaftUI-Minimap's micro buttons with it. So also `UnregisterAllEvents` on a blocked frame whose handlers reach outside it, unless something SaftUI needs runs on them. A blocked frame isn't on screen (the roleset API docs: `IsRolesetFiltered` is "hidden because of its rolesets"), so Blizzard's `Show` shouldn't run its `OnShow`; not yet confirmed in game.
9. **Don't override Blizzard globals or APIs**, not even for test data (e.g. faking `GetLootRollItemInfo` or `UnitExists`). Build SaftUI-owned preview frames instead (`SaftUI-Loot/roll.lua` `CreateRollPreviews`).
10. **Restoring fonts:** never `SetFontObject` with an object you got from `GetFontObject()` unless it's a named global Font. Restoring an engine-made Font made a region inherit from itself and crashed the client with a C stack overflow (`31afa68`, in the Ace skin, since removed). To put a font back, keep the named Font, or the file, size and flags from `GetFont()`.
11. **Blizzard's aura containers (unit frame buffs and debuffs) carry forbidden aspects** (`Blizzard_AuraContainer`):
    - The aura buttons forbid addon scripts on them and on their children (`UntrustedScriptExecution`), so a `HookScript` or a child frame's script on a button never runs.
    - The buttons also refuse tainted access while aura data is secret (`DenyTaintedAccessWhenAurasAreSecret`; see `C_Secrets.ShouldAurasBeSecret`), and so does everything on them, SaftUI's backdrop frame included. Blizzard applies it right after `PostCreateButton` (`AuraContainerUtil.ApplyAccessRestrictions`), so SaftUI styles a button as it's made, and again on a config update only while aura data isn't secret: in combat the restyle waits for the end of combat (`RestyleButtons` in `SaftUI-Unitframes/elements/auras.lua`). A frame made in combat gets its first update then (`UF.InitUnit`: a plate's first add, a group member joining), when its container has just made a batch of buttons; restyling them then errored in game, which stopped the frame's update and left its later elements unset (a plate's threat text had no font).
    - Blizzard sets each button's shown state, position and icon, and the container's size, through `secretwrap`. So `IsShown`, `GetRect` and `button.Icon:GetTexture()` return secret values to addons even out of combat. A boolean test on `IsShown()` errors: that's how SaftUI's aura tooltip overlay (`e3c6548`, backed out) failed. Addons can't tell which aura a button holds, or where it is.
    - Their tooltip, `AuraButtonTooltip`, is a forbidden frame hidden from addons: it can't be hooked or restyled.
    - A texture handed to a button (`AddDispelTypeTexture`, which oUF uses for `button.Border`) is held through its forbidden object table, so the container's `Show`, `Hide` and `SetVertexColor` calls on it never reach `hooksecurefunc` hooks. It also gets secret alpha, vertex color, tex coord and shown aspects. Addons can restyle and move it, but can't learn its color or whether it's shown. Hooking it to copy the dispel color onto the backdrop's edge (`ee0254f`, reverted) left the border uncolored.
    - The Cooldown Manager's items (`Blizzard_CooldownViewer`, both clients) carry none of these: no forbidden aspect and no access restriction in its XML or Lua, and no secure template. Its one secured piece is the viewer's map from aura instance IDs to items (`CooldownViewerSecure.lua`: a table that refuses tainted access), which SaftUI never reads. So its skin styles items as they're made, in combat too, and no cooldown value is read (they can be secret).
    - Once a container has an aura group, a frame anchored to it, or to anything anchored to it, must carry its layout aspect. Create it with `DisableUntrustedLayoutScriptsTemplate`, and never anchor a frame without that aspect, like `GameTooltip`, to it. That's why SaftUI draws no backdrop on the container itself: `UF:UpdateElement` skips the aura areas (`BARE_ELEMENTS` in `SaftUI-Unitframes/updaters.lua`). Each aura button still gets a backdrop of its own (`auras.lua`, `StyleButton`), anchored to a button the container lays out, as before themes; whether that holds on retail is an open in-game check (`docs/followups.md`, "Themes: in-game checks for what was only compile-checked").
12. **The Blizzard theme's art lives on SaftUI's frames.** Every piece (the tooltip border, the metal, a button's ring, a control's textures) is a texture of a frame of SaftUI's, kept in `st.ThemeArt`'s weak tables (`SaftUI-Core/themeart.lua`) and placed by anchors and offsets, never by a size read back from the game (sizes can be secret). The exceptions are Blizzard's buttons skinned by rule 1's recipe, whose ring and own-color piece are textures made on the button, so they lie between its icon and its texts (`st:SetTemplate(backdrop, theme, background, 'action', button)`): the totem bar's slot, summon and recall buttons, which are Blizzard's secure buttons (`SaftUI-Misc/totemskin.lua`), and the Cooldown Manager's items (a buff bar's icon frame), whose icon the skin moves to BACKGROUND under the ring (`SaftUI-Misc/Skinning/Blizzard_CooldownViewer.lua`). No field is written onto the button (the textures are kept in `st.ThemeArt`'s tables, keyed by SaftUI's backdrop), and the first draw is out of combat; an element changed in combat can make the own-color piece then, which is an in-game check (`docs/followups.md`, "Blizzard theme: in-game checks"). Nothing in SaftUI calls `NineSliceUtil` or a Blizzard setter to draw it (`tests/core/theme_blizzard_draw_test.lua` scans every SaftUI Lua file the game loads, outside `Libraries/`, for the name), and nothing of the theme's is written onto a frame that isn't SaftUI's; a frame that isn't is marked with `st:MarkForeign` before it's drawn. Where a control of Blizzard's already has its own art, SaftUI shows that art again instead of making a copy, and only through widget methods on regions whose alpha Blizzard never sets (`SetAlpha`, `SetDrawLayer`, `SetSize`, `SetPoint` on the region, none of them Blizzard's own Lua):
   - the buff skin's two borders (`SaftUI-Misc/Skinning/Blizzard_BuffFrame.lua`: `ShowBorder`);
   - the micro buttons' `Background`, `Shadow`, `PushedShadow` and performance bar, and their highlight (`SaftUI-Minimap/microbuttons.lua`: `StyleArt`);
   - the micro menu bar's own art is SaftUI's, not Blizzard's: each item's boxes are textures of its panel (kept in the item's entry in `micromenu.lua`'s `items`) and Forever's strip is a frame of SaftUI's in the bar's Container at the Container's level, placed through `st:SafeReposition`, so it follows the shown holders even in combat. Blizzard's `MicroMenu.BorderArt` and `BackgroundArt` stay faded and unmoved under every look (the strip replaces them), including in a vehicle or pet battle, where Blizzard moves its buttons out of the bar but the holders stay;
   - chat's edit box borders and focus textures, and an input box's `Left`, `Middle` and `Right` (`st:SkinEditBox`, `st:StyleEditBox`), and the scroll bars' own layers (`SaftUI-Core/Widgets/scrollbar.lua`);
   - the totem flyout's top and middle (`StyleFlyout`, `SaftUI-Misc/totemskin.lua`);
   - the bank's deposit button's `Left`, `Middle` and `Right` (`st:SkinButton`, `SaftUI-Core/Widgets/button.lua`).

   `st:SkinEditBox` conceals a box's border textures with `SetAlpha(0)` under Thick and Thin, not `st:Kill`, because `st:Kill` replaces `Show` (rule 1) and the textures must come back on a switch to Blizzard. Whether a region's alpha is ever set by Blizzard is checked against its source first (`.agents/clients.md`): the buttons' checked texture and the spell highlight are, so those are swapped, not faded (rule 1).
13. **Nameplates** (`SaftUI-Unitframes/nameplates/`, `.agents/architecture.md`). The game makes the plates (`NamePlate1`, `NamePlate2`, ...) and hands each one to unit after unit, and Blizzard's `NamePlateDriverFrame` runs them. On every add Blizzard's driver hands the plate a `UnitFrame` from its pool, which oUF blocks.
    - Nothing is written onto Blizzard's plate frames (the plate, its pooled UnitFrame, the widgets) beyond what the bundled oUF writes itself: its frame and style (`nameplate.unitFrame`, `nameplate.style`) and, on every add, blocking the pooled UnitFrame (`oUF:DisableBlizzard`: `UnregisterAllEvents` and `SetRolesets` on it and its bars). That is oUF's own departure from rule 1. The bundled oUF isn't patched for the plates, so an oUF update brings its driver as it is. SaftUI keeps its own state per plate in weak tables keyed by its frame on the plate (which plates show a unit, the target's art, the border owners) or by unit token (which plate a unit is on), and on its own frames.
    - `NamePlateDriverFrame` gets two post-hooks, on the frame itself since its mixin's methods are copied onto it (rule 2): `hooksecurefunc(NamePlateDriverFrame, 'UpdateNamePlateSize', ...)` sets SaftUI's size again after Blizzard sets its own, and one on `UpdateNamePlateOptions` puts the click areas back (below). Its events are left alone: SaftUI hears `NAME_PLATE_UNIT_ADDED`, `NAME_PLATE_UNIT_REMOVED` and `UNIT_FACTION` on a frame of its own, made after oUF's driver so oUF hears each add first. SaftUI also registers a callback with `CVarCallbackRegistry` on two of Blizzard's settings (`NamePlateConstants.SHOW_FRIENDLY_NPCS_CVAR` and `SHOW_ONLY_NAME_FOR_FRIENDLY_PLAYER_UNITS_CVAR`); the registry runs each callback on its own (`secureexecuterange`), so SaftUI's taints none of Blizzard's. Each hook and callback goes in only where the client has its name.
    - Blizzard's widgets on a plate, the pooled UnitFrame's `WidgetContainer` (a capture bar, a quest object's progress) and `SoftTargetFrame`, are moved onto the plate on every add with the widget methods oUF itself uses (`SetParent`, `ClearAllPoints`, one `SetPoint`), never with Blizzard's Lua. oUF moves them only at a plate's first add, for the UnitFrame it had then, and the pool hands the plate another one at each add. Reading `plate.UnitFrame` is only a read, and a UnitFrame without them (a spectator's) is left alone. `UF:RefreshPlateWidgets` places them again after SaftUI sets the friendly NPC plates setting, and on the frame after anyone changes it (Blizzard's own options, its key binding), since Blizzard anchors them again from that setting's callback, which the blocked UnitFrame keeps: oUF's `UnregisterAllEvents` drops its events, not its `CVarCallbackRegistry` callbacks.
    - The click area (the plate's hit-test points; ruling N11). oUF sets it to SaftUI's frame on every add that shows the unit (`ClearAllHitTestPoints`, then `SetAllHitTestPoints(frame)`). Blizzard's pooled UnitFrame sets its own again outside the add (`UpdateHitTestArea`: round its hidden bar and name, or none): from the driver's options update (`UpdateNamePlateOptions` runs `ApplyFrameOptions` on every plate: a window resize, the options loading, one of Blizzard's nameplate options) and from those two setting callbacks. `UF:RestorePlateHitTests` runs oUF's same two calls, and no other kind of write, for every plate SaftUI shows (never one the game took away or one showing only widgets), from the post-hook, at the end of `UF:RefreshPlateWidgets`, on the frame after either setting changes (`C_Timer.After(0)`: the registry's callbacks run in no set order, so Blizzard's can come after SaftUI's), and after the plate size is set (`UF:ApplyPlateSize`, out of combat only, like the size: a UI scale or display change, or Blizzard's own size, resizes the frames that fill their plates). `UF:RestorePlateHitTest(frame)` runs them for one plate after its frame's place or size changed (`UF:PlacePlateFrame`: a kind change, a box, a Width or Height), since whether the game's click area follows the frame's rect after the calls, or takes it when they're made, isn't said outright: `SetAllHitTestPoints`' doc ("Sets the anchor points ... to fully encompass the target region", `FrameAPINamePlateDocumentation.lua`, both clients) reads as anchors, which would follow. It goes through out of combat and on the add's tick; refused in combat (a `UNIT_FACTION` on a later tick), it waits for the end of combat with the others (below), and until then the plate clicks where its area was last set. In combat the game lets addon code change a plate's click area only on the tick the plate got its unit, or a tick on which Blizzard's own code changed it (`CanChangeHitTestPoints`, `FrameAPINamePlateDocumentation.lua`, both clients; the calls error otherwise). The post-hook runs on Blizzard's tick, so it goes through; SaftUI asks before each of its two calls (the docs don't say the exception stays open after an addon's first call), and the next frame's, or any refused, waits for the end of combat (`QueueAfterCombat('NameplateHitTest')`), and the plate clicks by Blizzard's layout until then. Whether nothing is blocked when each runs in combat is an in-game check (`docs/followups.md`, "Nameplates: in-game checks").
    - The game's nameplate settings and the plates' size are set out of combat only. The game refuses `C_NamePlate.SetNamePlateSize`, and some nameplate settings, from addon code in combat, so each waits (`st:QueueAfterCombat('NameplateSize')`, `'NameplateCVars'`). Blizzard's own size update runs untainted in combat; SaftUI's post-hook then waits for the end of it. oUF's driver sets the plates' hit test insets with every size (`C_NamePlateManager.SetNamePlateHitTestInsets`), and sets both once when it's made, at login, without waiting: whether a `/reload` in combat is then blocked is an in-game check. Blizzard's Lua never sets the insets (neither client's source calls it), so they stay oUF's, set again with every size SaftUI sets. While another nameplate addon draws the plates (`UF:NameplateAddon`), SaftUI makes no driver, sets and puts back nothing, and touches nothing of Blizzard's plates.
    - Forbidden plates (friendly plates inside an instance) are left alone: `C_NamePlate.GetNamePlateForUnit` doesn't hand them to addon code, so oUF builds nothing on one, SaftUI's listener skips it, and they stay Blizzard's.
    - A plate's frame takes no mouse: oUF turns it off, and `UF.ConstructUnit` registers no clicks and sets no `OnEnter` or `OnLeave` on it (a script could take the mouse over the plate), so a click on the plate reaches the game, which targets its unit. The frame isn't secure, so SaftUI updates, shows, hides, scales and rounds it in combat, and places and sizes it on its plate too (a friend's look is smaller and centred: `UF:PlacePlateFrame`), while the plate's click area is set to it. That nothing is blocked when a plate changes kind or size in combat is an in-game check (`docs/followups.md`, "Nameplates: in-game checks"): the stubs can't show whether the game then treats the frame as protected.
    - The pixel rounding (`UF.RoundLayout`, `nameplates/pixels.lua`) is SaftUI's own walk, from SaftUI's frame on the plate down through its regions and child frames. It never calls `PixelUtil.SetRoundLayoutToNearestPixelRecursively` and never reaches Blizzard's frames: the plate is the frame's parent, not under it. It skips, with everything under them, SaftUI's own aura containers by identity (`UF.auraContainers`, a weak-keyed set the aura element's constructor fills, checked before any method is called on the container), forbidden regions (`IsForbidden`), and as a fallback anything `IsObjectType('AuraContainer')` says is one (an XML intrinsic, which the game may not report that way). So the walk calls nothing on Blizzard's aura buttons (rule 11). What SaftUI makes on those buttons, each one's backdrop and Thick's inner debuff line, is rounded where `elements/auras.lua` makes it. `SetRoundLayoutToNearestPixel` and `SetIgnoreParentScale` are protected functions in their documentation, blocked only on a protected frame, which a plate's frame isn't; that nothing is blocked for plates made in combat is an in-game check (`docs/followups.md`, "Nameplates: in-game checks").
    - The target's plate is found by its frame (`C_NamePlate.GetNamePlateForUnit('target')` is the frame's parent), never by `UnitIsUnit`, which the game may answer with a secret for a plate's unit.
